# Let hydraulics, not spectra, choreograph water

Professional dancing-water shows do not map frequency bands to nozzles. Bellagio, Dubai, Grand Haven, Longwood and Fountain People all hand-place cues on a timeline against the audio and check them in a simulator, and one song takes days to weeks. The only fountain built directly on a 60–150 / 200–600 / 800–2200 Hz "colour organ" split was judged "an artistic disaster … just a jumping water". Academic work on automatic fountain choreography is thin: one peer-reviewed system, Yoo & Lee (ICMC 2009), builds a beat and structure grid and samples human-style scenarios from a Bayesian network. Its only reported benefit is authoring speed. The workable design for AquaFlow Studio is therefore an offline, event- and section-driven generator. It reads separated stems (drums → onsets and kick/snare/hat bands; bass → low-band envelope; vocals → pitch and presence; other → harmonic texture), places library motifs on a beat, bar and section grid, and then enforces per-actuator physics. Those physics dominate everything else: pilot valves take 50–190 ms to open and up to 1.3 s to close, VFD pumps ramp over 1–5 s, OASE swing drives top out at 60–72 °/s, a 10 m jet needs 1.43 s to reach its apex, and air shooters need 4–25 s to recharge. Each cue must therefore fire early by its own lead time. The timeline should present this as a 3-band-coloured waveform, per-stem frequency lanes and beat/bar/onset/section timing tracks, so a person can edit the first draft. The tool should not be sold as a finished-show generator.

## Every flagship fountain is hand-programmed beat by beat

The industry baseline is manual authorship. Wikipedia's survey notes that automated shows are still "painstakingly programmed by hand" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_fountain)). A TalTech thesis states that **"almost 100% of dancing fountains are pre-choreographed"**, and that each company uses proprietary time-domain editors whose programs are stored on the controller or streamed to it ([Satov, TalTech](https://digikogu.taltech.ee/en/Download/6b43eeb3-863d-48d1-b51f-5e0001868d41)). WET sketches Bellagio shows in its "Virtualwet" simulator. Its choreographer Claire Kahn says that **two minutes can take a week** and that the aim is "capturing the spirit … rather than being literal" ([ABC News](https://abcnews.com/Travel/las-vegas-bellagio-fountains-learn-dances/story?id=15167280)). At Dubai, **2–4 weeks per song** is normal: the show starts in simulation and is then tuned on site ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/lifestyle/dubai-fountain-the-stories-behind-the-music-1.698740)). Grand Haven's shows average **about 1,500 commands per song, and up to about 6,000**. Its sweeps are drawn from a fixed vocabulary: follow or oppose, long or short travel, hold or oscillate, at **7 discrete speeds** ([Grand Haven](https://www.ghfountain.org/technical)). Fountain People describe "manually choreographed beat-by-beat" programming, with chases timed to the score and water synchronised to crescendos ([Pool & Spa Marketing](https://www.poolspamarketing.com/trade/features/show-fountains-attract-visitors-choreographed-music-water-lights/2/)). Even Longwood's live DJ sets use pre-built "foundational fountain movements" and lighting looks, which operators layer and switch as the music changes ([Longwood Gardens](https://longwoodgardens.org/blog/2026-08-05/when-water-becomes-instrument)).

Real-time frequency following exists, but at the low end of the market. Chinese "声控" (sound-control) controllers split audio into 3 bands, or at most 6. In one documented 89C51 design, the mid band drives splash shapes ("snow pine", "fireworks") and the mid-high band drives rotating and swinging jets. Pulses closer than **0.8 s are ignored for pumps and 0.5 s for valves**, and a log lookup table converts music level into valve flow ([cnblogs](https://www.cnblogs.com/fm1029/p/15376612.html)). The patent record follows the same model: US 2,868,055 (band filters → pump height), US 4,376,404 (low/mid/high channels → spray groups), US 5,439,170 (**bands → 16 valves** plus lights) and US 6,276,612. US 6,276,612 matters most because it delays each band by **0–600 ms** to compensate for mechanical lag ([US2868055](https://www.google.ca/patents/US2868055); [US4376404](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/4376404); [US5439170](https://patents.google.com/patent/US5439170); [US6276612](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6276612)). The verdict on this approach is consistent. The TalTech prototype on 60–150 / 200–600 / 800–2200 Hz bands was "a total disaster … just a jumping water", and its author confined the method to idle mode ([Satov](https://digikogu.taltech.ee/en/Download/6b43eeb3-863d-48d1-b51f-5e0001868d41)). The Chinese patent CN103473423A likewise says automatically generated shows "look mechanical and lack artistry" ([CN103473423A](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN103473423A/zh)). Kahn's remark that the music should seem to follow the fountain points the same way: good choreography anticipates the music rather than reacting to it ([Review-Journal](https://www.reviewjournal.com/?p=1502215)). For AquaFlow, the band-follower belongs in an "ambient/idle" mode. Show generation should work from events and sections, and its output is a first draft for a person to edit.

## Analysis front end: seven bands, per-stem onsets, a beat grid and section labels

**Band edges.** The audio-engineering 7-band split is **sub-bass 20–60, bass 60–250, low-mid 250–500, mid 500–2k, upper-mid 2–4k, presence 4–6k, brilliance 6–20k Hz** ([Teach Me Audio](https://www.teachmeaudio.com/mixing/techniques/audio-spectrum)). WLED's light-organ uses 16 log-spaced channels between 43 Hz and 9.3 kHz, with a 512-point FFT at 22,050 Hz. Its pink-noise gain correction climbs from **1.70 on the lowest channel to 9.55 on the highest** ([WLED](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wled/WLED/main/usermods/audioreactive/audio_reactive.cpp)). That steep tilt is why each band must be normalised against its own running or song-wide maximum rather than one global maximum. The 512-point frame gives 43 Hz bins, which cannot separate sub-bass from bass, so AquaFlow should use a 2048-point STFT (21.5 Hz bins at 44.1 kHz) or larger. The instrument sub-bands below are engineering judgement, not sourced. For the drums stem: kick body 40–120 Hz; snare body 150–250 Hz plus crack 2–5 kHz; hats and cymbals above 6 kHz. Bass guitar sits at 40–250 Hz, and vocals have fundamentals at 80–1,100 Hz with presence at 1–4 kHz. On a full mix, vocals and bass cannot be isolated by band-pass filtering, which is why stems matter.

| Purpose | Recommended split (Hz) | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Per-stem frequency graph in timeline | 20–60 / 60–250 / 250–500 / 500–2k / 2k–4k / 4k–6k / 6k–16k | Teach Me Audio 7-band |
| 3-band waveform colouring | <250 / 250–2.5k / >2.5k | Engineering choice; rekordbox crossovers unpublished |
| Idle/ambient band-follower | 60–150 / 200–600 / 800–2200 | TalTech colour-organ prototype |
| Drums-stem triggers | kick 40–120; snare 150–250 + 2k–5k; hats >6k | Inference |

**Onsets.** Use log-magnitude spectral flux on a log-frequency filterbank. The SuperFlux reference uses **24 bands/octave over 30 Hz–17 kHz, a 3-bin frequency maximum filter, frame 2048 and 200 fps** ([SuperFlux](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CPJKU/SuperFlux/master/SuperFlux.py)). Dixon found spectral flux, phase deviation and complex-domain methods perform "similarly", so the simplest, spectral flux, is enough ([DAFx 2006](https://dafx.de/paper-archive/details/_kscRgr98aSFW4l-j1YJuA)). librosa's peak picker gives constants that port directly: **local max over 30 ms, local mean over ±100 ms, delta 0.07 on a [0,1]-normalised curve, wait 30 ms** ([librosa onset.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/librosa/librosa/main/librosa/onset.py)). Valves cannot use 30 ms separation, so raise `wait` to 50–100 ms for valve triggers. Sum flux within each drum sub-band to get separate kick, snare and hat onset tracks.

**Beats, bars and envelopes.** librosa's Ellis-style tracker estimates tempo by autocorrelation over an **8 s window with a log-normal prior centred at 120 BPM (σ = 1 octave)**. It then places beats by dynamic programming with **tightness 100** ([librosa rhythm.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/librosa/librosa/main/librosa/feature/rhythm.py); [librosa beat.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/librosa/librosa/main/librosa/beat.py)). Half-tempo and double-tempo errors are expected, so the editor needs tempo override and tap entry. For downbeats, assume 4/4 and pick the beat phase whose beats carry the most kick/bass onset strength (an unsourced heuristic). For envelopes, the light-organ convention is fast attack and slow release. WLED rises with 0.75·new + 0.25·old and falls over a 2 s default decay, and its limiter uses **80 ms attack and 1,400 ms decay** ([WLED](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wled/WLED/main/usermods/audioreactive/audio_reactive.cpp)). Offline, AquaFlow can do better: compute RMS at 100 fps, convert to dB, and map the −60…0 dB range (relative to the stem's 95th percentile) onto 0–1. Then smooth with τ_attack 30–80 ms and τ_release 300–1,500 ms. Pump curves need a longer release (0.5–2 s) than lights, because water has inertia.

**Pitch, timbre and structure.** pYIN on the vocals stem gives a melody contour. librosa's defaults are **frame 2048, hop 512, 100 thresholds, Beta(2,18) prior** ([librosa pitch.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/librosa/librosa/main/librosa/core/pitch.py)). In the browser, plain YIN followed by a 5–9-frame median filter and a voicing gate is a practical substitute. Spectral centroid and flatness are standard brightness and noisiness measures, but the notes cite no source for their formulas. For sections, Foote's checkerboard-kernel novelty on a self-similarity matrix of beat-synchronous chroma, MFCC and RMS finds the boundaries ([AudioLabs FMP](https://audiolabs-erlangen.de/resources/MIR/FMP/C4/C4S4_NoveltySegmentation.html)). Published kernels are around **32 beats** wide ([foote.py](https://code.soundsoftware.ac.uk/hg/segmentation/file/294f66d285af/foote.py)). EDM drop detection is an active topic ([Yadati et al., ISMIR 2014](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2014/paper/000297.pdf)). A workable heuristic, unsourced: flag a drop when the sub-bass/bass RMS jumps more than 6–10 dB within one beat after a low-bass build.

**Stems.** HT Demucs v4 reaches **9.0 dB SDR** on MUSDB-HQ (Hybrid v3: 7.7). It runs at about 1.5× track length and needs 3–7 GB of GPU memory. The code is MIT-licensed, but the repository is archived ([Demucs](https://github.com/facebookresearch/demucs)). Spleeter's 4-stem model (vocals/drums/bass/other) is MIT-licensed and "100x faster than real-time" on GPU ([Spleeter](https://github.com/deezer/spleeter)). Avoid Open-Unmix UMX-L in a commercial product, because its weights are **CC BY-NC-SA** ([open-unmix](https://github.com/sigsep/open-unmix-pytorch)). The licences of the Demucs and Spleeter model weights were not confirmed. When stems are unavailable, librosa-style median-filter HPSS (**kernel 31**) is a licence-free browser fallback. It separates percussive from harmonic content, not bass from vocals ([librosa decompose.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/librosa/librosa/main/librosa/decompose.py)).

## Valves, pumps, swing drives and gravity set the time budget

Hydraulic latency is ten to a hundred times larger than any onset-timing error. Tameson's reproduction of JP Fluid Control timings shows that **direct-acting ⅛–¼″ valves switch in 30/30 ms (open/close)**. Pilot-operated valves are asymmetric and slow down with size: **DN20 70–90 / 220–800 ms, DN25 80–100 / 250–800 ms, DN50 120–190 / 540–1,300 ms** ([Tameson](https://tameson.com/pages/solenoid-valve-response-time)). Fountain water-graphic valves are claimed to open in 2–5 ms ([Watershow](https://watershow.de/en/watergraphic/2g-96-watergraphic/)). A PLC tutorial specifies valves under 25 ms for 20 Hz frame operation ([Industrial Monitor Direct](https://industrialmonitordirect.com/blogs/knowledgebase/plc-musical-water-fountain-valve-control-implementation)). Fast closure has a cost. Closing faster than t_c = 2L/a produces the full Joukowsky surge Δp = ρ·a·Δv ([FIRGELLI](https://www.firgelliauto.com/blogs/calculators/surge-analysis-water-hammer-calculator)). With an assumed pressure-wave speed of about 1,200 m/s, a 20 m branch has t_c ≈ 33 ms, and stopping 2 m/s flow spikes about 24 bar. The generator should therefore stagger large-valve closures on one manifold by 20–50 ms, or ramp the pump down first.

Pumps and swing drives operate at phrase scale, not beat scale. FRECON's music-fountain inverter guide starts at **5 s accel / 5 s decel** over a 0–60 Hz speed range ([FRECON](https://www.frecon-inverter.com/en-solution/352.html)). By the affinity laws, head scales with speed squared, so 50% speed gives about 25% height. That implies a per-pump minimum-frequency floor, which must be calibrated on site. Small DC DMX pumps such as Fontana's VPA (200 W, 10 m head) are a separate, faster class ([Fontana](https://fontanafountains.com/products/spray-systems/special-water-effects/programmable-fountain-kit-with-dmx-pump-vpa-series/)), with vendor-claimed response of **20–50 ms** ([chinamusicfountain.com](https://chinamusicfountain.com/what-is-a-dmx-pump/)). OASE's 2D DMX swing drives reach **72 °/s (S and L) and 60 °/s (XL)** ([OASE MDD L](https://www.oase.com/en-AU/fountain-technology/multi-directional-drive-l-2d-dmx02); [OASE MDD XL](https://dev.oase.com/nl-NL/fountain-technology/multi-directional-drive-xl-2d-dmx02)). A 90° sweep therefore takes about 1.25 s, and swing amplitude per beat should be capped at ω_max × beat period × 0.8, about 29° at 120 BPM. Shooters are the slowest actuators: OASE AirNozzles need **4 s refill at 6–14 m, 6 s at 30 m and 25 s at 50 m**, giving 2–6 shots per minute ([OASE AirNozzle](https://www.oase-professional.com/en/family/f/airnozzle.1001037299.html)). Crystal's Titan quotes a 5 s recharge ([Crystal Fountains](https://crystalfountains.com/product/titan-tnn-series/)). At Dubai, the tallest "extreme shooters" are used least for exactly this reason ([Wikipedia: Dubai Fountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubai_Fountain)).

Gravity sets the remaining limit. Ideal rise time is t_up = √(2h/g) ([arXiv 0801.4160](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0801.4160)). A jet can reach its apex within one beat of period T only if **h ≤ gT²/2 (1.23 m at 120 BPM)**. It can rise and fall within one beat only if **h ≤ gT²/8 (0.31 m)**. An open-source scheduler fires 10 m jets (1.43 s to apex) "over two seconds earlier" than the cue. It uses tall nozzles for phrases and short ones for pulse, at roughly one cue every ⅓ s ([5d-fountain-song-show](https://github.com/Yoo-yipeee/5d-fountain-song-show)). Combining these figures gives the compensation rule every cue needs: **lead = t_valve_open + t_column (+ t_fill if the branch drains) + k_drag·√(2h/g)**, with k_drag ≈ 1.1–1.3 to be calibrated from video. Add the ramp time for VFD cues, the travel time for swing cues, and the close latency plus about 2·t_up of visual drain for cut-offs. The drag factor and column terms are derived, not measured. Syncronorm calibrated Depence's simulated heights and motor speeds against site video for the same reason ([Syncronorm Van Phuc](https://www.syncronorm.com/newsitem/van-phuc-city-fountain-created-with-depence-r3)).

| Actuator class | Open / response | Close / recovery | Fastest repeat | Default lead to apex |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Water-graphic valve, 0.5 m | 2–25 ms | 2–25 ms | ~20–25 Hz | ≈0.35 s |
| Direct-acting DN8–15 | 30 ms | 30 ms | ~8–15 Hz | 0.03 s + t_up |
| Pilot DN25, 3 m jet | 80–100 ms | 250–800 ms | ~1–3 Hz | ≈1.1 s |
| 10 m jet | — | — | — | ≈1.8–2.0 s |
| VFD pump | 1–5 s ramp | 1–5 s ramp | 1 swell per 2–4 s | ramp + t_up |
| 2D swing (OASE) | 60–72 °/s | — | ±15° ≈ 0.42 s | Δθ/ω + t_flight |
| Air shooter 20–50 m | — | 6–25 s refill | 2–6 shots/min | ≈2.2–3.5 s |

## Map stems to nozzle families and song sections to pattern changes

No manufacturer publishes a rulebook, but the qualitative pairings are consistent across sources. Powerful jets "resemble drums or brass" ([fountains.com](https://fountains.com/aquatic-harmony-the-art-of-music-and-water-choreography-in-fountains/)). Swing nozzles give "continuous, flowing motion", while digital jets chop the stream ([SyncD Connect](https://www.syncdconnect.com/post/how-to-build-musical-fountain)). Fog "fills in the background" ([WaterShapes](https://watershapes.com/fountain-fascination/)). A large loudness jump should "shoot from all the nozzles" ([Satov](https://digikogu.taltech.ee/en/Download/6b43eeb3-863d-48d1-b51f-5e0001868d41)). WET's Peter Kopik warns that unrelentingly intense music must not exhaust the audience ([Washington Times/AP](https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2019/apr/1/game-of-thrones-takes-over-bellagio-fountains-in-l/)). Grafting the stem features onto the actuator limits above gives the following default rule set. It is a synthesis of those sources, not a published standard.

| Stem / feature | Fountain family | Implementable rule |
|---|---|---|
| Drums: kick onsets (40–120 Hz) | Central jets, small shooters, DC-pump surges | On downbeats or strong kicks, height = log-mapped onset strength; shooters only on section downbeats and drops, with cooldown ≥ refill |
| Drums: snare / hat onsets (150–250 + 2–5k; >6k) | Solenoid chasers, jumping jets | One chase step per 8th note (snare) or 16th note (hats); rotate valves round-robin so each valve respects its min period (≥0.5 s for pilot valves) |
| Bass stem: 20–250 Hz smoothed envelope | VFD pump height of main ring/curtain | Height ∝ √(envelope) (affinity law); τ_release 0.5–2 s; slew-limit to the drive ramp; never below min-frequency floor |
| Vocals stem: pYIN f0, voiced prob > 0.5 | 2D/3D swing nozzles, laminar arcs | Pitch → tilt angle (pitch-to-tilt is unsourced); presence 1–4 kHz energy → swing amplitude; capped at ω_max·T·0.8 |
| Other stem: centroid, flatness, chroma | Mist, fog, curtains; lighting hue | High flatness → mist/fog; centroid → colour temperature; chroma change → candidate bar line |
| Full-mix loudness jump (>6–10 dB in 1 beat) / drop | All-jets burst, white flash | Pre-ramp pumps by ramp time; fire on downbeat with each family's lead |
| Sustained high energy | Rotate motif families | Change family every 4–8 bars; reserve tallest effects for climaxes |

Tempo should drive motion through quantised musical units rather than continuous scaling. Grand Haven's seven sweep speeds are the precedent ([Grand Haven](https://www.ghfountain.org/technical)). The suggested defaults (inferred): one swing oscillation per bar at 60–100 BPM and per two beats above that, provided the swing angle fits ω_max; chases at 8th or 16th notes; waves traversing once per one or two bars. Pattern families change at 4-, 8- or 16-bar phrase boundaries and at detected section boundaries. Longwood's practice of switching looks as "tempos, rhythms, and musical keys" change supports this ([Longwood Gardens](https://longwoodgardens.org/blog/2026-08-05/when-water-becomes-instrument)). Lighting follows the water rather than leading it. Fountain People adjust intensity and colour together with the water, beat by beat ([Pool & Spa Marketing](https://www.poolspamarketing.com/trade/features/show-fountains-attract-visitors-choreographed-music-water-lights/2/)). Grand Haven runs fades, strobes and "twinkles" on 43 RGB fixtures ([Grand Haven](https://www.ghfountain.org/technical)). A US lighting patent maps tempo to colour and amplitude to brightness ([US9763311](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9763311)). No source supports a fixed key-to-colour convention such as minor = blue. AquaFlow should therefore assign one palette per section label (warm for high energy, cool for low), crossfade slowly on pads, flash white on drops, and tie brightness to the water-height envelope. LEDs need no lead time, so they should lag the water's lead by its full hydraulic latency and land exactly on the beat.

## Draft generation as motif retrieval over a beat grid, scored like dance synthesis

The one fountain-specific generator, Yoo & Lee, extracts onsets, beats and structure, then samples scenarios from a **Bayesian network trained on human-authored examples**. It reports no quality metric beyond being faster than manual work ([Yonsei CGA](https://cga.yonsei.ac.kr/26fb79e7-b116-81ba-bc1b-ece97eed427b)). Dance synthesis provides the stronger template. Fukayama & Goto concatenate motion segments under **beat-wise connectivity constraints** and reuse choreography across self-similar sections ([AIST SMC 2015](https://staff.aist.go.jp/m.goto/PAPER/SMC2015fukayama.pdf)). ChoreoGraph turns beat-aligned segments into graph nodes and scores transitions on style, tempo and smoothness ([HKUST](https://researchportal.hkust.edu.hk/en/publications/choreograph-music-conditioned-automatic-dance-choreography-over-a/)). Light-O-Rama's "Instant Sequence" generates effects from timing marks plus a theme of allowed colours and movements ([LOR help](https://www.lightorama.com/help/instant_sequences.htm)). Its users find it hit-or-miss on "complex songs with notes all over the place" ([LOR forum](https://forums.lightorama.com/topic/29083-instant-sequence/)). MIT's lighting thesis found that learning from concert video failed and fell back to a hand-designed abstraction over pre-analysed audio ([MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/147314?show=full)).

From these sources, the AquaFlow generator can be specified without deep learning, which has no fountain dataset equivalent to AIST++ to train on. The pipeline:

1. Build the beat, bar and section grid.
2. Tag each library motif with its energy level, length in bars, the stem it responds to and its actuator classes.
3. For each section, retrieve motifs whose tags match the section's RMS, onset density and centroid.
4. Give every repeat of a section label the same motif, mirrored or varied.
5. Solve transitions with Viterbi over bars. The cost terms are pump-ramp feasibility, valve-state compatibility, shooter cooldown and a repetition penalty.
6. Apply per-actuator lead times and hard constraint clamps.
7. Learn a Markov transition table from the user's own hand-edited shows. This is the lightweight version of Yoo & Lee's approach.

The dance field's metrics adapt directly for evaluation. AIST++ introduced the **Beat Alignment Score** ([AI Choreographer](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.08779)). Ground-truth dance scores only **about 0.24**, against Bailando's 0.23 and EDGE's 0.26–0.27, so the target is human-level alignment, not 1.0 ([InfiniteDance](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.13375); [Liner review](https://liner.com/review/edge-editable-dance-generation-from-music)). The EDGE authors found that FID correlated weakly with perceived quality, so listener A/B tests remain the gold standard ([EDGE supplemental](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/supplemental/Tseng_EDGE_Editable_Dance_CVPR_2023_supplemental.pdf)). AquaFlow should compute four fountain metrics:

| Metric | Definition | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Water BAS | Mean of exp(−d²/2σ²), σ ≈ 50–100 ms, between apex/edge times after latency and music beats | Near the score of hand-made shows |
| Energy match | Correlation of music loudness with aggregate flow over each section | High within each section |
| Structure consistency | Choreography similarity for same-label sections minus similarity across different labels | Positive |
| Diversity | Motif entropy per minute | High enough to avoid a single repeated look |

Metrics 2–4 and the σ for water BAS were not found in any fountain source. They are adaptations from the dance literature.

## Timeline: banded waveform, stem lanes and re-snappable timing tracks

Choreographers read music on a timeline as a coloured waveform above rows of timing marks. rekordbox's official colour schemes are **3-band (low blue, mid orange, high white)** and **RGB (low red, mid green, high blue)** ([rekordbox](https://rekordbox.com/en/feature/djing/)). xLights stacks Beat, Onset, Chord, lyric and Vamp-plugin timing tracks under the waveform, and supports tap entry with the "t" key ([xLights manual](https://manual.xlights.org/xlights/chapters/chapter-four-sequencer/timing-tracks)). Two user problems are documented. In xLights, placed effects "have no relation to the original timing marks", so a corrected grid leaves cues stranded. Vamp beats can also drift late in a song ([AusChristmasLighting](https://auschristmaslighting.com/threads/copy-beat-timings-to-end-of-song.15094/)). For long songs in a browser, BBC's peaks.js renders precomputed min/max peaks on canvas at samples-per-pixel levels such as **[512, 1024, 2048, 4096]** ([peaks.js](https://github.com/bbc/peaks.js)). waveform-data.js can build those peaks with Web Audio ([waveform-data.js](https://github.com/bbc/waveform-data.js)).

The recommended AquaFlow layout, from top to bottom:

1. A song overview with a viewport box.
2. A 3-band-coloured detail waveform (<250 Hz, 250–2.5 kHz, >2.5 kHz).
3. A section lane, colour-matched across repeats.
4. Bar and beat lanes.
5. An onset lane with a sensitivity slider.
6. One lane per stem. Each shows the 7-band energy as a stacked or heatmap strip normalised per band, with the stem's own onsets overlaid. This is the "per-stem frequency graph".
7. Cue lanes per fountain group, drawn at both the trigger time and the predicted apex time, so lead compensation is visible.

Precompute everything in a worker with an OfflineAudioContext or a JS STFT. Store per-band RMS in a min/max pyramid and draw only the visible tiles. Cues should keep a reference to the timing mark that placed them, so they re-snap when the grid is corrected. A global "nudge marks by N ms" control is still useful for calibration. This layout is engineering inference from the sources above; no fountain editor's UI (Depence, WinDMX, WEPS) was documented in the notes.

## Conclusion

The research turns the product question around. "Map frequencies to nozzles" is the method the industry abandoned. The defensible version uses frequency content only to decide when an event happens and which stem it belongs to. Hydraulics decide which actuator can play it and how early it must fire, and musical structure decides which motif family plays. Combined with a grid that a person can edit, that is novel enough to compete with Depence-class hand authoring on speed without claiming to replace it. The research does not show that fountain generators are scarce because the problem is hard. The evidence points instead to fountain physics, which make naive mapping look bad, and to the absence of a training corpus.

The largest open uncertainties are physical, not musical. No source gives measured jet-front rise times, VFD height step responses or per-channel delay fields in commercial tools. AquaFlow's per-device lead table and drag factor should therefore ship as calibratable fields, fed by a high-frame-rate video test of each nozzle class on site. Every show a user hand-corrects can also feed motif transition statistics back into the generator, so its drafts improve with each corrected show.
