# One phase formula drives every fountain wave

Nearly every wave a fountain can show, whether it comes from physics, the ocean, a stadium crowd or a lighting console, reduces to a single expression: **L(i,t) = lo + (hi − lo) · form(t/T − φ_i)**. Here *form* is a one-cycle shape (sine, bump, pulse, saw, attack-decay), *T* is a period that should be counted in beats or bars, and *φ_i* is a per-jet phase taken from the jet's position along a line, its angle on a ring, or its distance from a point on a grid. This is exactly how grandMA2, grandMA3 and Avolites define effects: a form between a low and a high value, a speed in BPM, and a phase in degrees spread across the fixtures, remapped by groups, blocks and wings ([MA help](https://help.malighting.com/grandMA2/en/help/key_effects_predefined.html); [grandMA3 Phasers](https://help.malighting.com/grandMA3/2.3/HTML/phaser.html); [Avolites](https://manual.avolites.com/docs/effects/shape-generator)). Fountain makers publish no comparable effect library. Shows are keyframed by hand or in Depence and WET's VirtualWET, so the console model is the best template available ([Syncronorm](https://syncronorm.com/products/depence2/visualization/fountains); [Wikipedia: Dubai Fountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubai_Fountain)). AquaFlow's eight field kinds already cover the core 1-D travelling, standing, swell, sweep, crossing, asymmetric-crest and accelerating waves. The big gaps are of three kinds. First, there are **no true 2-D geometry waves**: radial ripple by real distance, spirals, interference, wakes and moving spots. Second, there is **no smooth noise** for the organic "lake, aurora, chop" family. Third, there are **no onset-triggered single-shot events** such as plucks, bursts, surf breaks and ringdowns. Water also needs tempo rules in its own right. A solenoid jet can complete at most one cycle every 2 s, which is a full bar at 120 BPM. A proportional valve that takes 2–3 s to cross its full range can follow a full-height sine only if the period is about 6–9 s, roughly 2–4 bars. Water should therefore carry bar-scale and phrase-scale motion, and the lights should carry the beat-scale detail using the same formula.

A note on evidence. Statements with a citation come from the research notes. Formulas for fountain use, parameter ranges and music pairings are engineering synthesis built on the cited maths and are marked *(inference)* where it matters. Facts about AquaFlow's code come from `wavePatterns.js`, `famousPatterns.js` and `plcLimits.js` in this repository. The 1 s solenoid minimum and the 2–3 s proportional ramp are the brief's planning assumptions, not sourced values. Real solenoid valves open in tens to low hundreds of milliseconds ([Tameson](https://tameson.com/pages/solenoid-valve-response-time)), so 1 s is a conservative visual and hydraulic limit, not a property of the valve.

## Consoles solved phase distribution; fountains add dwell and slew

The lighting consoles agree on a small parameter set, and it ports directly to jets. In grandMA2, **Phase** is "the time offset that individual fixtures use within the effect", entered as a range such as 0 Thru 360. **Width** is the duty share: 20% PWM means high for 20% of the cycle. **Groups** "divides the phases according to the number of groups". **Blocks** makes "neighbouring fixtures run the same phase". **Wings** "applies the phase starting from the outside and moving inward". **Single Shot** runs an effect once ([MA help](https://help.malighting.com/grandMA2/en/help/key_effects_predefined.html)). grandMA3 builds effects from steps instead of forms. Each step has a Width given as a percentage of a beat (100% = 0.5 s at 120 BPM), a Transition share, Accel/Decel curve shaping, and a Measure that sets beats per loop ([grandMA3 Phasers](https://help.malighting.com/grandMA3/2.3/HTML/phaser.html)). The PWM recipe is Transition 0 with Width 50 ([grandMA3 Width](https://help.malighting.com/grandMA3/2.3/HTML/keyword_width.html)). That step model is exactly an on/off solenoid chase. In Avolites, Spread n is equivalent to a phase step of 360/n ([Avolites forum](https://forum.avolites.com/viewtopic.php?p=4424)).

From these definitions an engine needs one phase-index function *(inference)*. Blocks first: j = floor(i/B). Then groups: j = j mod G. Then wings: mirror every odd wing. Then the phase itself: φ_i = φ₀ + (φ₁ − φ₀)·j/(M − 1), or j/M on a closed ring. AquaFlow already implements part of this. `grouped()` moves jets in groups and gives alternate groups a half-cycle offset (`opposite`). `bothWays` and the `from-point` path give 2-wing mirroring. Distance from the origin, *s*, gives the rolling-from-the-tip family. What is missing is **interleave** (odd/even sub-selections), **more than two wings**, and **phase measured in real space** rather than by member rank. `memberGeometry()` reads x and y only to choose the origin. Every kind then works on rank i, p = i/n and index distance s, so a ring of uneven spacing, two concentric rings or a grid cannot show a true radial or angular wave.

Fountain hardware adds two rules that lighting never needs, and the right response to each is different.

The first rule governs **solenoids**. With a minimum on-time and off-time of t_min, a pattern is legal only if duty·T ≥ t_min and (1 − duty)·T ≥ t_min, so at 50% duty T ≥ 2·t_min. Crucially, **spatial speed is not limited**. A crest may race across many jets per second as long as it is wide enough that each jet dwells at least t_min. The right rendering is therefore threshold, then hysteresis, then a minimum-dwell filter, and smooth shapes become moving lit blocks *(inference)*. AquaFlow's `plcLimits.js` already applies `min_on_ms` and `min_off_ms`, but it does so after the fact. It stretches short openings and merges short gaps, which is safe but changes the look. A wave that is solenoid-aware should choose its width and period so the filter never fires.

The second rule governs **proportional valves and VFDs** with a slew limit s, given as a fraction of full range per second. A sine of amplitude A passes undistorted only if its steepest slope, πA/T, stays within s. That means T ≥ πA/s: a full swing needs about **6.3 s with a 2 s ramp and 9.4 s with a 3 s ramp**. A faster sine collapses into a smaller triangle wave whose peak-to-peak is about s·T/2 *(inference from the maths in [Wikipedia: Square wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_wave_(waveform)) and the brief's slew figures)*. The triangle is the most valve-friendly shape, because its slope is a constant 2/T. Sharp edges become ramps of 2–3 s.

Water also lags. A column takes roughly √(2h/g) to rise: about 1.4 s to reach 10 m and about 3.1 s to reach 48 m, ignoring drag. Hits must therefore be fired early by the valve delay plus the rise time ([Tameson](https://tameson.com/pages/solenoid-valve-response-time) for the valve part; the kinematics are standard). AquaFlow already uses a lead of 0.1 + 1.2·√(2h/g).

Space is sampled too. A wave needs at least 2 jets per wavelength to avoid aliasing and about 4 to look smooth, so the number of visible crests k should not exceed about N/4. In practice, **1–4 crests across the array read best at audience distance** *(inference)*.

Those limits translate into tempo rules once the beat length b = 60/BPM is known. At 120 BPM, b = 0.5 s and a bar is 2 s. Solenoid water then needs T of at least ceil(2/b) beats, which is 1 bar, while a chase can still step one jet per beat as long as the lit block spans at least 2 beats of travel. Full-height proportional motion needs T of 2–4 bars. Smaller amplitudes can move at 1 bar. Lights can run at ⅛ beat. The phase should be locked to the downbeat, φ = (t − t_downbeat)/T − k·p, so crests arrive at the end of the array exactly on beat 1. A crest traverse of 1–2 bars reads well *(inference)*. AquaFlow's famous looks already convert bars to seconds through `barSec`. The field kinds themselves still store `period` in seconds, though: rolling defaults to 6.67 s and stadium to 7.5 s. Storing the period in beats would make every kind tempo-safe by construction.

## Twenty-one mathematical waves, ranked by how well water can show them

The physics and maths catalogue uses one convention. Position along a path is p ∈ [0,1]. On a ring, p wraps and the angle is θ = 2πp. Grid coordinates are (x, y) ∈ [0,1]², and r is the distance from a source point. The travelling phase is φ = t/T − k·p, measured in cycles. A bipolar wave w ∈ [−1,1] maps to a level by L = 0.5 + 0.5·A·w. The textbook forms come from the cited sources. The fountain adaptations are *(inference)*.

The building blocks below are sourced. A standing wave is the sum of two equal waves travelling in opposite directions. It produces fixed nodes spaced λ/2 apart and does not travel ([Wikipedia: Standing wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_wave)). Two close frequencies beat at |f₁ − f₂| ([Beat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beat_(acoustics))). A linear chirp has phase 2π(f₀t + ct²/2), and an exponential chirp has phase 2πf₀T(k^(t/T) − 1)/ln k ([Chirp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chirp)). A damped oscillation follows A·e^(−ζω_n t)·cos(ω_d t) ([Damping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damping)). A KdV soliton is −(c/2)·sech²[(√c/2)(x − ct)]: taller solitons move faster and are narrower ([KdV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korteweg%E2%80%93De_Vries_equation)). The Burgers equation steepens a wave front until it breaks ([Burgers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgers%27_equation)). Two sources interfere with an amplitude of 2A·|cos(Δφ/2)| ([Interference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave_interference)). The modes of a circular membrane are J_m(α_mn·r/a)·cos(mθ), with α₀₁ = 2.405 and α₁₁ = 3.832 ([Membrane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vibrations_of_a_circular_membrane)). A truncated Fourier square wave overshoots by about 9% (the Gibbs phenomenon), and Fejér weighting removes the overshoot ([Gibbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibbs_phenomenon)). A Poisson process has exponential gaps of mean 1/λ ([Poisson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_point_process)). Bounce heights fall as e^(2n) ([Restitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coefficient_of_restitution)). Simplex noise gives smooth, deterministic randomness without grid artefacts ([Book of Shaders](https://thebookofshaders.com/11/)). The shaping functions expImpulse, cubicPulse, pcurve and gain come from Quilez ([iquilezles](https://iquilezles.org/articles/functions/)).

The heartbeat row exposes a real bug. The current Heartbeat look draws lub at 6% and dub at 24% of one bar, which is 0.36 s apart at 120 BPM. On a valve-mode jet, `plcLimits.js` merges the two pulses into one longer opening, so the audience sees a single thump. Splitting lub and dub across two alternating jet groups fixes it *(inference)*.

Readability at distance depends far more on scale and contrast than on mathematical subtlety *(inference)*. Travelling sines with k ≤ 2, block chases, standing-wave alternation, single humps, ring rotations, radial ripples and saw sweeps read at any jet count from about 20 up. Triangle, beats, Lissajous, noise, bounce and chirp need about 40 jets or proportional valves. Interference, Chladni, Fourier harmonics, damped ringing and double-pulse heartbeat need a grid of about 10×10 or more, or lights. Bitmap water curtains confirm the pattern: drawing fine detail takes many fast actuators, and one blog claims <25 ms valves across about 80 nozzles ([Industrial Monitor Direct](https://industrialmonitordirect.com/blogs/knowledgebase/plc-musical-water-fountain-valve-control-implementation), unverified).

## Nature and crowds supply recognisable asymmetry, grouping and endings

Audiences recognise natural waves less by their physical values than by four cues *(inference)*: **asymmetry** (sharp crests, a steep front, fast rise and slow fall), **grouping** (sets, whelps, ring packets), **direction and ending** (a trigger cluster, a drawback, a single front, a collapse) and the **ratio of crests to array size**. The sourced numbers below set the shape. Their periods must then be time-scaled to the basin.

Ocean swell is trochoidal. Its free surface has "sharper crests and flat troughs", deep-water waves of λ = 100 m have T ≈ 8 s, and the limiting steepness is about 0.318 ([Trochoidal wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trochoidal_wave)). Deep-water group velocity is half the phase velocity, so crests are born at the back of a set and die at the front ([Capillary wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capillary_wave)). This is the physical recipe for **swell with sets** *(inference)*: u = ((1 + cos φ)/2)^p with p of about 1.5–2.5 for sharp crests, multiplied by an envelope 0.5·(1 + cos(Δk·x − Δω·t)) with Δω ≈ ω/7, giving about 7 waves per set. Chop is a JONSWAP-weighted sum of 8–16 sines with peak-enhancement γ = 3.3 ([ffpack](https://ffpack.readthedocs.io/en/latest/moduleCookbook/lsmWaveSpectra.html)). WLED's Lake and Pacifica effects are cheap versions of the same idea: two or four layered sines at different wavelengths, with a moving threshold or whitecaps added above a summed brightness ([WLED FX.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wled/WLED/main/wled00/FX.cpp)).

Surf has four breaker types. A plunging crest "curls over" and "drops onto the trough", releasing its energy at once, while spilling breakers bleed it slowly ([Breaking wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breaking_wave)). That makes surf an event, not a loop. A front travels through the riser, the jets at the break line spike on beat 1 of the drop, and a low, jittery "whitewater" level then decays over 1–3 s *(inference)*. Tidal bores are single fronts: the Qiantang reaches 9 m at about 11 m/s, the Severn up to 2 m. They come in two forms, breaking (a roller) and undular (a smooth front followed by "whelps") ([Tidal bore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidal_bore)). For a fountain, the bore is a step front that leaves a raised plateau behind it, with 3–6 decaying whelps. It suits a key change or a new section that should hold its height. Tsunamis begin with a drawback in which the sea recedes for minutes before the ridge arrives ([Tsunami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsunami)). On water this becomes a breakdown in which every jet drops to its floor, then a long hump with no visible crest spacing that grows toward the "shore" end. That is the shape of a cinematic build into a finale. Ripples from a stone are gravity-capillary waves with a minimum phase speed of about 0.23 m/s ([Capillary wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capillary_wave)). On a fountain they become 3–5 expanding crests with amplitude falling as 1/√r. A Kelvin wake keeps its arms at **19.47° to the track whatever the speed** ([Kelvin wake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelvin_wake_pattern)), which makes a V trailing a moving point that can follow the melodic contour. A seiche is a standing wave with period T = 2L/√(gh) and harmonics at T/2 and T/3 ([Seiche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seiche)). It is the physical name for what AquaFlow's seesaw already does.

The crowd and biological waves add **excitable-medium** behaviour, meaning a trigger, a travelling pulse and a refractory tail. Farkas, Helbing and Vicsek modelled La Ola with excitable-media models ([arXiv cond-mat/0210073](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0210073)). Wikipedia's summary reports about 12 m/s (≈22 seats/s), about 15 seats wide, started by a few dozen fans and usually running clockwise ([Wave (audience)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave_(audience))). The brief cited about 20 seats/s, and the paper's text could not be checked, so treat the exact figure as uncertain. For a fountain, the takeaway is that each seat stands and sits in roughly 0.7 s, and that **the refractory time must exceed the active time**, which is what keeps the wave moving one way. A cellular-automaton version (rest, active, refractory, firing when weighted active neighbours pass a threshold) adds what AquaFlow's deterministic stadium kind lacks: a visible trigger cluster, waves that sometimes die out, and laps that repeat *(inference)*. Starling agitation waves run at about 13.4 m/s, faster than the flock's own 10.6 m/s, and are carried by changes in orientation, not density ([BirdGuides](https://www.birdguides.com/articles/waves-of-starlings/); [Hemelrijk et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4564680)). On a fountain that becomes a dark band flashing across a slowly morphing noise field. Honami waves over wheat glide downwind faster than the mean canopy wind ([APS DFD 2023](https://meetings-archive.aps.org/dfd/2023/zc43/1)). On a fountain they become Gaussian dips moving across a field, with each jet springing back like a damped oscillator. Pulsating aurora rises and falls over 2–20 s, with observed patch periods of 4 s and 7 s ([arXiv 2209.14545](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.14545)), which is noise drift plus slow patch pulsing. Fireworks contribute burst-and-decay templates: a peony stops abruptly, a chrysanthemum leaves trails, and a willow must hang for at least 10 s ([fireworks.com](https://fireworks.com/safety/glossary)). Flames puff at about 10 Hz ([arXiv 2112.07240](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.07240)), so a flame effect for water must be slowed to about 1–3 Hz on lights and run slower still on jets.

## Pixel engines show that every effect is a short closed form

WLED's FX.cpp is the most portable reference. Each effect is a short function of time, pixel index, speed and intensity ([WLED FX.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wled/WLED/main/wled00/FX.cpp); [WLED effects](https://kno.wled.ge/features/effects/)). Running is sin8(i·x_scale − counter). Theater lights the pixel when i mod width = aux, a step chase. Scanner and Sinelon are a ping-pong head with a fading trail. Phased subtracts a moving threshold from cubicwave8, which turns a smooth wave into a variable duty cycle. That is exactly the right trick for solenoids. Bouncing Balls integrates h = (½gt + v₀)t and multiplies v₀ by 0.9 − i/n² on each bounce. Starburst has a 250 ms ignite, a 1,500 ms fade and decelerating, mirrored fragments. Ripple spawns rings at random positions with a triangle rise and a linear decay, and Ripple Peak triggers them from audio peaks. xLights' Wave effect has a compact parameter set (type Sine, Decaying Sine, Square, Triangle or Fractal; number of waves; thickness; height; speed; direction; mirror) and animates any parameter with value curves ([xLights manual](https://manual.xlights.org/xlights/effects/off/wave)). That value-curve idea maps onto AquaFlow's `grow` and `accel` envelope.

Measured against these catalogues, AquaFlow already has the WLED families Running (travelling), Theater (seesaw and the alternate path), Scanner and Sinelon (sweep), Ripple (lotus bloom and the ripple preset) and Twinkle (rain). It lacks the **layered-sine "Lake/Pacifica"** family, **Bouncing Balls and Popcorn**, **Starburst-style mirrored bursts with a decay**, and **audio-peak-triggered ripples**.

On the fountain side, the hardware sets the vocabulary. Bellagio runs 208 oarsmen, 798 mini-shooters, 192 super-shooters and 16 extreme-shooters with 4,792 lights. Every nozzle is programmed individually, and two minutes of music can take a week to choreograph ([Bellagio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fountains_of_Bellagio)). Chasing nozzles make low arcs "that meander throughout the fountain" ([WaterShapes](https://watershapes.com/?p=32542)), and Chinese suppliers name effects after nozzles: peacock, fan, windmill, swing, rolling swing ([Himalaya](https://www.aajjo.com/product/big-music-fountain-in-bursa-himalayamusicfountain)). Shooter waves are event chases whose speed is simply the step time. Oarsman sways are sines on the pan angle, with the phase spread along the row and a speed cap of 2πfA. For example, ±30° at 0.5 Hz needs about 94°/s *(inference)*.

## Map music parts to time scales, not to waveforms

No source links particular wave visuals to particular musical features, so the mapping below is design judgement *(inference)*. It is consistent with how consoles bind speed to BPM and size to masters, and with fountain systems that read "beat, volume and intensity" ([WaterShapes](https://watershapes.com/shall-we-dance/)). The organising principle is **time scale**. Each music layer gets the wave family whose natural period matches it, and the hardware class able to render that period.

**Bass and low end** suit long, low waves on proportional water: swell with sets, travelling sine at k = 1, standing wave mode 1, and noise flow. Amplitude follows low-band RMS. Periods run 2–4 bars.

**Vocals and melody** suit waves with one clear moving subject: a soliton or single hump, sweep, Kelvin wake, a Lissajous spot, sawtooth runs, or a stone ripple whose source position follows pitch (low notes on the left, high notes on the right). The subject should move with the phrase, about one traverse per 2–4 bars.

**Drums and hits** suit triggered single shots: radial ripple from a point, pluck, firework bursts, surf breaks, and see-saw swaps on the backbeat. On water these fire early by the rise lead. On lights they land exactly on the hit.

**Hi-hats** suit Poisson sparkle with density tied to onset rate, rendered on lights with a refractory version on solenoids.

**Whole-song energy** should drive amplitude, coverage and contrast (the hi value and the share of jets active) rather than speed.

**Builds** suit chirps that land on the downbeat, Fourier sharpening on lights, and a tsunami drawback. **Drops** suit radial ripple, surf breaks, fireworks and sparkle bursts. Sections change form, phase spread or wings at their boundaries. The console idea of keeping phase continuous when tempo changes, grandMA3's Sync ([grandMA3 Phasers](https://help.malighting.com/grandMA3/2.3/HTML/phaser.html)), should carry over so that a pattern never jumps at a tempo map change.

## Conclusion

The research shifts the design question from "which wave shapes should AquaFlow add" to "which time scale and which hardware class each layer of the music belongs to". The shapes are cheap: each is a line or two of closed-form maths, and WLED shows that an entire effect catalogue fits in short functions. What makes a fountain wave look professional is putting the right period on the right actuator. Solenoids carry wide, fast-travelling blocks with dwell of 1 s or more. Proportional water carries 2–4 bar envelopes. Lights carry the beat-scale detail of the same field, so water and light read as one gesture. Spatial speed is free on any hardware, so the most effective way to make water look fast is to move wide crests quickly across many jets, not to switch each jet faster.

Two uncertainties should be closed with measurements before the defaults are frozen. No source pinned down the real minimum on/off time of fountain solenoids, VFD ramp times, or the hydraulic rise lag of a jet. The 1 s and 2–3 s figures used here are planning assumptions, and the guards should read per-device calibration values. No study tested which wave visuals audiences associate with which musical parts. Running the A/B rating loop AquaFlow already has across the top five effects above would turn those pairings from judgement into evidence.
