# Plan the arc, search the phrases, compile the physics

AquaFlow Studio can produce professional-quality automatic water shows for any site and any song, but not by refining its frequency-to-nozzle rules. It needs a three-level pipeline of the kind that has shipped in dance and lighting tools. First, a **show-arc planner** turns the song's structure and energy into per-section targets: intensity, how much of the fountain plays, which nozzle families are held back for heroes, colour, and planned rests. Second, a **phrase search** (dynamic programming or beam search) chooses one look per 4–16-bar phrase from a site-adapted pattern library by minimising a scoring function. Third, a **constraint compiler** turns the chosen looks into valve, VFD, servo and DMX channels that the hydraulics can actually perform. This architecture needs no training data, which matters because no public dataset of fountain shows paired with music exists. The one academic fountain generator, a Bayesian network from 2009, was criticised for lacking large-scale coherence, the same flaw dance research fixed with a global-then-local hierarchy. Professionals spend days to weeks per song, so a credible draft in minutes is the product. The research also changes three assumptions in AquaFlow's current design. Section labels from any detector are wrong about 20–25% of the time, so energy rank, not the label, should drive the arc. Several best-in-class music models (madmom's weights, Essentia's mood models, MERT, MuQ and therefore SongFormer) carry non-commercial licences and cannot ship in a sold app. The fountain's own hydraulic topology, which nozzles share a pump and which switch on their own solenoid, belongs at the centre of generation, not in a post-filter. The plan at the end is phased. Phase 0 (about two weeks) measures today's generator. Phases 1–2 (about 3 months, all in the existing JavaScript) add the site model, arc planner, phrase search and compiler. Later phases add licence-clean Python deep analysis, a calibrated GPU water simulator and learning from user ratings.

A note on evidence: statements with a citation come from the research notes. Statements marked *(inference)* are engineering synthesis. Numbers marked *(unverified)* came from background knowledge or secondary sources that could not be confirmed. Facts about AquaFlow's code and the Lebu test site come from the project itself and are labelled *(AquaFlow)*.

## Professionals choreograph an arc and cast nozzles as instruments; they do not follow volume

The craft literature is thin but consistent. Show designers start with song choice and favour pieces with "crescendos – both progressive and sudden". They label the song A/B/C (intro, verse, chorus, final, plus bridge, coda and solos) and give each section its own "sequence or pattern" ([Forme d'Acqua](https://formedacqua.com/en/innovation-and-technology/the-programming-of-musical-dancing-fountains/)). Vendors describe the same **five-part arc: opening, build-up, climax, transition, ending**, with high central jets for climax points, flame for "dramatic sections, transitions, and final climaxes", and air explosions used "selectively for impact" ([Huaxin](https://huaxinfountain.com/musical-fountain/)). Hangzhou's West Lake fountain cut its pieces to **under about 5 minutes**, because organisers judged audience freshness to last 3–4 minutes ([Hangzhou News](https://hznews.hangzhou.com.cn/chengshi/content/2016-04/12/content_6127566_2.htm)). WET's chief choreographer Peter Kopik frames the job as matching the fountain's "energy" and "drama" to what the music means to its audience. He says the hard case is a score that is "continuously energetic", which must stay exciting without exhausting viewers ([AP via Washington Times](https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2019/apr/1/game-of-thrones-takes-over-bellagio-fountains-in-l/)). He also treats beat feel as cultural: in Arabic music "you are actually bending your knee at the beat, not jumping up" ([Wikipedia: Dubai Fountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubai_fountain)). Hero hardware is rare partly by design and partly by physics. Bellagio's super-shooters must refill a 200 psi air receiver after each shot, "so they can only fire so frequently" ([phcppros](https://www.phcppros.com/articles/2835-an-engineering-marvel)). Dubai's tallest extreme shooters are "used least" because they take long to build pressure ([Wikipedia: Dubai Fountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubai_Fountain)).

Role casting is the second constant. Vendors map **moving or robotic jets to "the lyrical and melodic movements"**, staccato vertical jets to tempo, jumping jets to bass, shooters and air bursts to accents, high central jets and flame to climaxes, and mist to smoothing transitions ([Forme d'Acqua](https://formedacqua.com/en/innovation-and-technology/the-programming-of-musical-dancing-fountains/); [SafeRain](https://saferain.com/en/blog/how-to-make-a-dancing-fountain/); [Huaxin](https://huaxinfountain.com/musical-fountain/)). Forme d'Acqua's orchestra metaphor makes the crown nozzle "the singer", the rings the guitarist and drummer, and the jumping jets the bass. It adds that **"in the next song the roles are reversed"**, so casting should rotate between songs ([Forme d'Acqua](https://formedacqua.com/en/innovation-and-technology/the-programming-of-musical-dancing-fountains/)). Spatial movement is authored, not emergent. WET's patent maps every nozzle into a 2D UV image and animates textures at about 24 fps, with pixel brightness 0–255 as nozzle state. It separates shooter-type nozzles (refilled → firing → refilling) from continuous nozzles (startup → looping → dying → dead) ([US11813629B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11813629B2/en)).

Lighting follows the same structure. Fountain vendors insist that light is choreographed with the water, not added as decoration ([Huaxin](https://huaxinfountain.com/musical-fountain/); [SafeRain](https://saferain.com/en/blog/how-to-make-a-dancing-fountain/)). Concert-lighting practice adds usable rules: give the song a primary colour, find the high point (which may not be the end), expect **4–5 looks per song**, and make each cue "musical punctuation" ([ProSoundWeb](https://www.prosoundweb.com/pro-production-approaches-to-cueing-the-music-with-concert-lighting/2/)). The biggest chorus is lit as the brightest point ([ProSoundWeb](https://www.prosoundweb.com/story-telling-production-lighting-to-match-the-arc-of-a-song/)). Bellagio uses only white light ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fountains_of_Bellagio)), so "more colour" is not the same as "better".

The automation verdict is blunt. Yoo & Lee wrote that automatic choreography from simple features such as volume is "seldom used" because the results are "prosaic" ([Yonsei CGA](https://cga.yonsei.ac.kr/26fb79e7-b116-8195-a463-d4cece8f3352)). Summaries of their own Bayesian-network generator say it **lacked large-scale coherence**, which pushed them toward pattern-based methods ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/40020915_Creating_Musical-Fountain_Shows)). The bar for "professional" is high: Bellagio took up to **a week for about 2 minutes** of music ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fountains_of_Bellagio)), and a vendor blog quotes 20–60 hours for a 5-minute show with 500–2,000 cues ([Huiqi](https://www.huiqifountain.com/blogs/brand/dmx-fountain-control-system)).

The craft gaps are as important as the rules. No source gave numbers for the share of nozzles active per section, how often looks change, or how long rests and blackouts last. Any such numbers in AquaFlow are design choices to be tuned with the evaluation harness, not industry standards. From the 4–5-look rule, a look change roughly every 30–60 seconds with beat-level accents inside each look is a reasonable default *(inference)*. Placing a darker, sparser moment before each hero hit is likewise inferred from lighting practice, not sourced for fountains.

Mapped onto the Lebu observations *(AquaFlow)*, the gaps are craft gaps, not signal-processing gaps. A show where switched areas are active only about 35% of the time, with no planned build to a climax, no rests and section-coloured lights, is exactly the "string of disconnected pre-programmed sequences" that modern tools try to avoid ([SafeRain](https://saferain.com/en/blog/how-to-make-a-dancing-fountain/)).

## The site must be modelled as a typed hydraulic graph, not a list of 0–100% channels

Actuators differ by orders of magnitude in how fast they can change, and the generator has to know which kind each output is. **Solenoids switch in roughly 30–60 ms** (blog source) ([FirgelliAuto](https://www.firgelliauto.com/blogs/mechanisms/luminous-fountain)). The earlier AquaFlow research found pilot valves at DN25–DN50 closing in 250–1,300 ms ([Tameson](https://tameson.com/pages/solenoid-valve-response-time)). OASE requires rotating nozzles to take **no less than 3 s from 0 to 100% height** ([OASE](https://www.oase.com/en/water-technology/rotating-nozzle-5-30-e-int)). The OASE AirNozzle 50 E reaches 30–70 m with a **25 s refill** ([OASE](https://www.oase.com/en-au/fountain-technology/airnozzle-50-e-50-m-vertical-01)). The OASE 2D drive pivots 200° by 140° ([OASE MDD L 2D](https://www.oase.com/nl-NL/fountain-technology/multi-directional-drive-l-2d-dmx02)), and the earlier research found its speed at 60–72 °/s ([OASE MDD L](https://www.oase.com/en-AU/fountain-technology/multi-directional-drive-l-2d-dmx02)). Large 2D nozzles use two servo axes with homing at power-on ([INVT](https://www.invt.com/solutions/application-of-servo-system-in-large-music-fountain-113)). A typical RGBW underwater spot uses **6 DMX channels** (R, G, B, W, strobe, dimmer) ([OASE ProfiLux](https://www.oase.com/en/fountain-technology/profilux-led-xl-rgbw-spot-dmx02-int)). Gravity adds its own delay: a jet needs about √(2h/g) to reach its apex, about 1.0 s at 5 m and 1.4 s at 10 m ([arXiv 0801.4160](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0801.4160) for h = v²/2g; times computed). After a valve closes, the water already in the air still has to fall.

Coupling is where AquaFlow's current model is thinnest. Three topologies dominate *(inference from sources)*: one VFD pump per effect, giving independent proportional height; one pump feeding a manifold of solenoids, where **opening more valves drops pressure and height on the others**; and hybrids. On a poorly sized header, the first nozzles take most of the flow and downstream ones starve ([Industrial Monitor Direct](https://industrialmonitordirect.com/blogs/knowledgebase/spray-wash-header-hydraulic-design-calculations)). Dry-fountain research treats jet timing as a pump-scheduling problem ([UPB Scientific Bulletin](https://scientificbulletin.upb.ro/static/pdfs/rez5e3_690095.pdf)). Pump affinity laws (Q ∝ n, H ∝ n²) are standard engineering rather than fetched *(background)*. They imply that height scales roughly with the square of VFD frequency, so a linear height slider should map to frequency through a square root. This explains two Lebu symptoms *(AquaFlow, inference)*. Baskets on a shared pump cannot be choreographed independently, because each one changes its siblings' height. Switched areas sit idle because the current generator gives each area one music part and lets each jet be claimed by only one part, so a single active stem leaves most switched areas unused. The fix is for the generator to plan **per hydraulic source**: budget Σ open-nozzle flow ≤ Q_max(f), schedule coupled nozzles as one unit, and feed the VFD forward from a commissioned "frequency versus open-valve count" table to hold height when valves open and close.

Wind and audience position are the site facts AquaFlow ignores today. Splash extends roughly **one jet height horizontally in calm air**, and Safe-Rain recommends a wind gauge that cuts the tallest jets while the rest keep running ([Safe-Rain](https://saferain.com/en/blog/tips-of-fountain-design-part-2/)). Commercial controllers reduce height in **3 stages or off** ([OASE](https://www.oase.com/en-US/fountain-technology/wind-dependent-fountain-control-3-k-int)) with anemometer thresholds adjustable over 0–25 mph ([Roman Fountains](https://landscapearchitect.com/ladetails/landscape-product/fountains-equipment/roman-fountains-corp./rwc-sm-dul-wind-control)). Bellagio cancels shows at 25 mph ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fountains_of_Bellagio)). WET's Aria fountain switches between "high" and "low" versions of each show on wind ([Las Vegas Sun](https://lasvegassun.com/news/2009/dec/16/water-swirling-spewing-frozen-entertain-visitors-c/)). No vendor publishes default set-points. A starting curve of reduction from about 4–5 m/s, a floor at about 8 m/s and tall jets off at 10–11 m/s is *unverified*, and must be site configuration. For the audience, no fountain-specific method to infer "front" was found. AquaFlow assumes +y. A scored heuristic, offered for user confirmation, is the practical answer *(inference)*. In priority order, it checks: explicit seating or terrace layers; the side facing the largest open public area; the side opposite a building backdrop; the side with the most footpath near the basin; and the normal to the long axis of linear nozzle arrays.

Groups can be inferred automatically when the design does not name them. RANSAC fits circles from 3-point samples and lines from 2-point samples ([treeX arXiv 2509.03633](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.03633); [Fischler & Bolles](https://static.aminer.org/pdf/PDF/000/387/686/a_ransac_based_approach_to_model_fitting_and_its_application.pdf)). Mirror symmetry can be detected as a registration problem ([arXiv 1611.05971](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1611.05971)). A practical pipeline clusters by nozzle type, runs DBSCAN on positions, fits rings, arcs and lines to get a natural chase order, hypothesises mirror axes from same-type point pairs, and lets **shared valves or VFD tags in the PLC I/O list override geometry**, because a hardware group is a coupled group *(inference)*. For interchange, GDTF (DIN SPEC 15800:2022) and MVR (DIN SPEC 15801:2023) are the open standards for fixtures and scene layout ([GDTF spec](https://github.com/mvrdevelopment/spec/blob/main/gdtf-spec.md); [GDTF-Share](https://gdtf-share.com/help/)). No fountain-specific nozzle exchange format exists, so nozzle data still comes from PDF datasheets such as OASE's height, flow and pressure tables ([OASE Comet datasheet](https://www.oase-livingwater.com/fileadmin/dp/media/images-cb/DOK/PRD/DB/DOK_PRD_DB_50959-Comet3-5T_%23SEN_%23AQU_%23V1.pdf)).

## A licence-clean music stack exists, but separation dominates the time budget

Understanding the music means four things: a reliable grid (beats, downbeats, meter), structure (sections and their energy roles), stems with their events, and mood. The choice of models is constrained as much by licence as by accuracy, because AquaFlow is sold commercially.

Two numbers decide the user experience. AquaFlow's JavaScript analysis takes about 20 s for a 4-minute song *(AquaFlow)*. HTDemucs on CPU would take about 6 minutes for the same song, or about 4.5 minutes with ONNX *(computed from the cited ratios)*. The right design keeps the current JS path as **"fast mode"**, so the first draft appears in under 30 s. It then runs a background **"deep analysis"** in the bundled Python engine, caches the result by song hash, and re-plans when it lands. The backend's `requirements.txt` currently has `torch` and `librosa` commented out *(AquaFlow)*, so onnxruntime is the lighter dependency to bundle *(inference)*. The ONNX parity of HTDemucs's STFT ops was not verified.

Labels deserve less trust than they get today. SongFormer, the 2025 state of the art, reaches only about 0.80 label accuracy, and All-In-One 0.74 ([SongFormer paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02797v2)). Even Gemini 2.5 Pro scores 0.748 with poor boundary precision (HR.5F 0.42) in the same benchmark. A fountain show tolerates a wrong label but not a misplaced climax. So the planner should rank segments by smoothed loudness and stem density, treat the top-ranked segments as chorus or drop whatever their label, and use labels only to group repeats *(inference)*. There is no permissively licensed modern drop detector. The academic baseline is Yadati et al. ([ISMIR 2014](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2014/paper/000297.pdf)), so build-ups and drops should be heuristics: rising loudness and onset density over 4–16 bars, then a bass-and-loudness jump on a downbeat at a boundary *(inference)*.

Non-Western meter is a known gap, not a solvable detection problem today. Training data skews to 3/4 and 4/4, downbeat detection leans on Western chord-change cues ([arXiv 2509.11241](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.11241)), and **no beat-tracking evaluation on Ethiopian music was found**. beat_this has no DBN 3-or-4-beats-per-bar constraint, which favours compound meters in principle ([beat_this paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.21658)), but this is untested on chik-chika-style 6/8. Human-in-the-loop correction gave **2–5× F-measure gains** on Candombe and Bambuco with minimal annotation ([ISMIR 2021](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2021/paper/000065.pdf)). AquaFlow therefore needs a beats-per-bar selector (3, 4, 6 or 12), a tap or nudge for tempo and downbeat, and a "felt pulse" setting: in 6/8, accents fall on the dotted-quarter pulse, not on every eighth note *(inference)*. Following Kopik, it should also have a style switch for accent direction, rising on the beat versus dropping ("bending the knee") on the beat. Twenty to fifty annotated Ethiopian and Arabic songs would serve as a validation set and, under beat_this's MIT licence, a fine-tuning set *(inference)*. Energy- and stem-driven choreography is largely culture-agnostic; only the grid is at risk.

## Generation should be hierarchical search over a library, with physics enforced last

The dance and lighting literature converges on one lesson: local models make locally plausible, globally aimless output. **Lodge** (CVPR 2024) fixes this with a global stage that produces "characteristic dance primitives" and local stages that fill detail ([Lodge](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/html/Li_Lodge_A_Coarse_to_Fine_Diffusion_Network_for_Long_Dance_CVPR_2024_paper.html)). Learned end-to-end generators such as EDGE, Bailando and Lodge need hours of paired motion capture, and no fountain equivalent exists. The most transferable system is **ChoreoMaster**, which shipped in several NetEase games and was accepted by professional artists. It runs a graph search over a library of phrases under "various choreographic rules" ([ChoreoMaster](https://cg.cs.tsinghua.edu.cn/papers/SIG-2021-ChoreoMaster.pdf); [3dvf](https://3dvf.com/en/siggraph-2021-choreomaster-can-make-cg-characters-dance)). Its follow-up describes the cost as a **beat score plus a structural penalty against excessive repetition**, solved by **dynamic programming offline or beam search in real time** ([arXiv 2503.20218](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.20218)). Lighting tells the same story. The learned Skip-BART model beat a rule-based emotion→colour mapping in a 38-rater study (4.35 versus 2.67 on a 7-point scale). Yet it was trained on only 699 rock-heavy samples, predicts hue and value for a single light, and its authors admit "weaker long-term rhythmic structure" ([arXiv 2506.01482](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.01482v2)). Commercial auto-sequencers for xLights are analysis-plus-templates ([xlightsseq](https://xlightsseq.com/posts/9331/)). The conclusion for AquaFlow is a mostly deterministic hierarchy, with learning added only where small data suffices.

**Level 1: show-arc planner** (`arcPlanner.js`). Input: sections with energy rank, build-up and drop flags, and the SCM. Output: a **Show Plan** with one entry per section that sets an energy target, a coverage target (share of hydraulic sources and areas active), a layer budget (1–3 simultaneous layers, all layers only at the climax, *inference*), the hero families allowed and their shot budget (respecting cooldowns), a rest or contrast marker before the climax, a palette and look count, and a role casting of nozzle families to stems that rotates per song and seed. The arc template is the five-part opening → build → climax → transition → ending, with the climax placed at the highest-energy section, which "may not be the end" ([ProSoundWeb](https://www.prosoundweb.com/story-telling-production-lighting-to-match-the-arc-of-a-song/)). Repeats of a section label get the same look family, mirrored or varied. An optional **LLM director** can write the same JSON from a compact text description of the music and the site, the pattern DanceChat uses to turn an ill-posed one-to-many mapping into an interpretable plan ([DanceChat](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.10574v2)). It must be enum-constrained to real area and family IDs, never emit timestamps finer than a phrase, and always have the rule planner as an offline fallback *(inference)*. No published, evaluated music→LLM→fountain system was found.

**Level 2: phrase search** (`phraseSearch.js`). Phrases are 4, 8 or 16 bars, snapped to downbeats and section boundaries. Each state is a tuple of look (a pattern template from `musicPatterns.js`, `famousPatterns.js` or the custom library), role casting, intensity level and spatial variant (mirror, direction, area subset). Beam search with width K ≈ 32–64, or exact Viterbi when the state space is small, minimises a weighted cost *(inference)*:

| Cost term | What it rewards or penalises |
|---|---|
| Music fit | Onset density ↔ pattern speed, stem activity ↔ role, energy ↔ height and coverage (today's `choosePattern` table becomes this prior) |
| Arc tracking | Deviation from the section's energy, coverage and layer targets |
| Coverage | Penalty for idle switched areas and idle sources when the plan wants them active (fixes the ~35% problem) |
| Transition | Ramp feasibility between phrases, pump reuse, visual continuity, no water-hammer pile-ups |
| Repetition | n-gram repeat of looks, ChoreoMaster-style; structure-consistency bonus for same-label repeats |
| Hero discipline | Shooter and flame use outside planned moments, cooldown proximity |
| Spatial balance | Front/back layering versus viewpoint; mirror symmetry by default, asymmetry allowed in verses |

The state space is tiny: roughly 15–40 phrases per song, each with a few hundred candidate states. Search therefore runs in well under a second in a worker, and seeded top-k sampling yields "Variant A/B/C" for the user *(inference)*.

**Level 3: beat-level rendering.** Inside each chosen look, the existing renderers do the fine work: chases ordered by the SCM's `chaseOrder`, waves as moving textures over nozzle positions in WET's UV style ([US11813629B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11813629B2/en)), and accents from kick, snare and hat onsets and the bass envelope. Smooth edge easing and the ±15% peak step stay.

**Level 4: constraint compiler** (`showCompiler.js`, growing out of `hardwareFollow.js` and `plcLimits.js`). It is the sole authority on physics. It enforces min on/off times, VFD ramp limits and pre-ramps, per-source flow budgets with open-count feed-forward, coupled groups scheduled as one unit, shooter cooldowns, servo deg/s and range, the wind clamp, the splash envelope against the basin polygon (including tilt, since a 45° jet reaches about twice as far as a vertical one rises, *physics inference*), and staggered closures on a shared manifold. It also applies per-output lead times. AquaFlow's lead of 0.1 + 1.2·√(2h/g) already matches the drag-corrected rule from the earlier research. Lights get no lead, so they land exactly on the beat. The compiler emits **editable timeline tracks per group** that keep a link to the plan and the timing mark that created them, because pros refine drafts rather than accept them ([OASE/Depence feature list](https://vandarkitekterne.eistrupweb.dk/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Show-programmering-DMX.pdf)).

**How the frequency-mapping approach should evolve.** The earlier report already concluded that band-following is what the industry abandoned. TalTech's 3-band prototype was "just a jumping water" ([Satov, TalTech](https://digikogu.taltech.ee/en/Download/6b43eeb3-863d-48d1-b51f-5e0001868d41)). The new evidence sharpens where each piece of today's code belongs. `stemBands.js` (7 bands at 20 Hz) stops choosing patterns and becomes a **feature layer** with three jobs: event detection (kick, snare and hat onsets per band), phrase descriptors (band-energy profiles that feed the music-fit cost), and micro-modulation inside a look (height breathing, brightness). Direct band-to-nozzle following survives only as an "ambient/idle" mode. The `choosePattern` rule table becomes the music-fit prior in the search. `designRows`' one-part-per-area casting becomes the planner's role casting, extended so that a single area can play different parts in different sections and so that roles rotate between songs. The 13 famous looks stop being inserted "every other phrase" and become library entries with tags (energy, layers, required families, hero use) that the search picks when the plan calls for them. `MusicLanes.jsx` stays as the human view of the same features.

## Simulation must be driven by compiled channels and calibrated per nozzle

A preview only helps the user judge a show if it shows what the hardware will do. That means the simulator should consume the **compiled** channels (valve spans, VFD curves, servo angles, DMX), not the generator's intent, which AquaFlow's as-built preview already does in part. Physically, the ideal height v²/2g overstates real jets. Trettel defines a height efficiency η = h/H, which experiments found to depend on a Froude-type parameter ([Trettel 2015](http://www.trettel.us/pubs/2015/Trettel-2015-Theoretical-range-and-trajectory-of-a-water-jet.pdf)). Historical practice notes that bores too small "fall back as vapour or mist" ([Gardenvisit](https://www.gardenvisit.com/book/gardening_tools_equipment_and_buildings/chapter_7_edifices_(for_storage_bees_ice_shelters_etc)/water_supply_for_fountain_jets)). A Lueger-type correction h = H/(1 + φH), with φ depending on bore, gives η ≈ 0.69 for a 10 mm bore at H = 20 m, but the coefficient is *unverified* and should be a per-nozzle calibration parameter. Jets break up through Rayleigh, wind-induced and atomisation regimes ([Phys. Rev. Fluids](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRevFluids.5.044001)), and correlations for breakup length disagree with each other ([KoreaScience](https://koreascience.kr/journal/OMHHBZ/v18n1.pub)). So a good real-time model is an intact column to a calibrated breakup length, then drops with quadratic drag relative to the wind, with η fitted so the simulated peak matches the datasheet or measured height *(inference)*. Mist drops (10–100 µm) fall so slowly that wind dominates them, so mist should be advected sprites or a volume, not ballistic drops *(inference)*.

Dynamics matter more than droplet detail for judging choreography. Emitting each particle with the speed implied by the instantaneous pressure, v₀(t) = Cv·√(2ΔP(t)/ρ), reproduces the bunched "cap" at a valve opening, the hanging collapse after a close, the n² height ramp of a VFD, and the lagging curved streak of a swinging nozzle, all without special cases *(inference)*. The patent record confirms why this matters: large flows "require large cutoff periods to avoid problems associated with the water hammer effect", which makes water harder to sync than light ([US4844341](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4844341)).

Performance is not the obstacle. Raw WebGPU has simulated and rendered **10 million particles at about 63 fps on a GTX 1060** ([TU Wien](https://cg.tuwien.ac.at/research/publications/2023/PETER-2023-PSW)). CPU particle updates bottleneck around 50k, while compute shaders reach millions ([Utsubo](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/threejs-best-practices-100-tips)). three.js's WebGPU renderer falls back to WebGL2 ([Utsubo](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/webgpu-threejs-migration-guide)). A full FLIP solver is offline-only even in Houdini, where fountains are split into smaller sims ([SideFX](https://www.sidefx.com/tutorials/how-to-simulate-a-small-fountain/)). A Lebu-sized site of about 140 nozzles at 2–5k live particles each needs well under 1 M particles. The design that follows *(inference)*: instanced ribbon or tube meshes for intact columns, one pooled GPU particle buffer integrated in a compute pass, spawn-on-impact splash, a mist sprite layer, LOD by distance, and a deterministic seek (particles as a function of emit time and seed, or about a 4 s pre-roll after scrubbing).

For lighting, water drops scatter strongly forward. Henyey-Greenstein, or the sharper Jendersie-d'Eon fit for droplets, is the real-time standard ([Maxon](https://help.maxon.net/c4d/en-us/Content/html/What_is_Volumetric_Rendering.html.md); [UW thesis](https://digital.lib.washington.edu/researchworks/items/fdd6d0c2-e3fa-4914-9735-62177b690370)). Because AquaFlow already pairs each light with a nozzle, each jet only needs its own one to three fixtures, which avoids a many-light problem. Add a per-nozzle "aeration" blend from clear (refractive) to foam (white, isotropic), and HDR with bloom and ACES or AgX tone mapping, without which saturated LEDs on foam look washed out *(inference)*.

The same parcel model in Python can drive wind footprints and offline export *(inference)*. RK4 integration of sampled parcels per nozzle with a wind vector, histogrammed into a landing grid, gives the overspray fraction and the wind-safe height curve that the compiler needs. The existing OpenVDB, Alembic and Blender exports can then share one physics "ground truth". No public fountain footage dataset exists, so calibration is home-grown. Film each nozzle class side-on at 60–240 fps with a scale reference, extract tip height over time, and fit delay, v₀, η and drag at several VFD speeds to confirm n² scaling. This mirrors how sprinkler-drop equations were fitted to measured trajectories ([ASCE 1989](https://ascelibrary.com/doi/10.1061/%28ASCE%290733-9437%281989%29115%3A2%28156%29)) and how Syncronorm calibrated Depence against site video ([Syncronorm](https://www.syncronorm.com/newsitem/van-phuc-city-fountain-created-with-depence-r3)).

## Measure quality with gates, metrics and blind A/B ratings

Without a reference dataset, FID-style metrics are meaningless. Dance's Beat Alignment Score is contested: EDGE argues it is "fundamentally flawed" because it penalises good dances that skip beats ([EDGE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.10658)). Human ratings therefore remain the gold standard. Skip-BART supplies a small-scale recipe: objective curve-tracking metrics plus **38 raters on a 7-point scale over 6 dimensions**, analysed with repeated-measures ANOVA, and a cross-domain test on unseen genres ([arXiv 2506.01482](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.01482v2)). AquaFlow's harness should have three layers *(inference)*.

The first layer is **hard gates**: zero violations of min dwell, ramp, flow budget, cooldown, servo range, splash envelope at the configured wind, and PLC limits. Any violation fails the show.

The second layer is **automatic metrics**, computed by `showMetrics.js` from the compiled channels after latency, on the same timeline the audience sees:

| Metric | Definition | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Event alignment | Share of water and light change events whose apex lands within ±1 beat-tolerance window (σ ≈ 50–100 ms) of a beat, onset or boundary; tolerant of skipped beats | Musicality |
| Energy tracking | Correlation of per-bar loudness with "visual water mass" (Σ height × flow) | Dynamics |
| Arc fidelity | Climax section has the highest water mass and coverage; a contrast dip precedes it; final section ≥ 80% of climax | Show shape |
| Coverage | Share of time each hydraulic source and area is active, by section; idle-but-available share | Fixes 35% problem |
| Structure consistency | Look similarity across same-label repeats minus across different labels | Coherence |
| Diversity | Look entropy per minute; n-gram repeat rate; distinct looks per song (target 4–8) | Variety |
| Hero discipline | Hero shots per minute, all inside planned moments | Restraint |
| Peak usage | % of climax time at ≥ 70% design height | Impact |

The third layer is **human A/B**. Render shows from the simulator to video, run blind pairwise comparisons (new versus current generator, versus a hand-made reference, versus the variant seeds), and fit Bradley-Terry or Elo scores on musicality, variety, climax and overall. Use 10–20 songs across genres, including at least 3 Ethiopian 6/8 songs, and two or three sites (Lebu plus a small and a linear site). A regression suite of fixed songs and sites stores metric snapshots and fails CI on a drop. Over time, the A/B outcomes fit the cost weights of the phrase search. Tens of comparisons are enough to tune a dozen weights, while a learned music-to-look embedding in the ChoreoMaster style would need hundreds to thousands of labelled phrases *(inference)*.

Two sequencing choices are deliberate. Phase 0 comes first because every later claim of "professional quality" needs the scorecard and the A/B loop. Phases 1–2 come before deep analysis because the Lebu weaknesses are planning and hydraulics problems that better stems would not fix *(inference)*. Phase 3 can run in parallel with Phase 2 if a second person is available.

The main risks are known. The licence status of HTDemucs weights and CLAP needs a legal confirmation. The rule-based arc can feel formulaic, which the seeded variants, role rotation and Phase 5 tuning address. Default wind thresholds and swing speeds are unverified until commissioned on site. Ethiopian meter detection may stay weak, which is why manual meter controls ship in Phase 1 rather than waiting for a model.

## Conclusion

The research turns "automatic fountain shows" from a signal-processing problem into a planning problem with a physics compiler. The music analysis AquaFlow already has is roughly sufficient for a good first draft. What is missing are the two layers professionals carry in their heads and Lebu exposed: a show-level arc that spends height, coverage, hero shots and darkness deliberately, and a model of the site as coupled hydraulic sources rather than independent channels. The field's own evidence points the same way. The one fountain generator to rely on local probabilities lost large-scale coherence, while the production-proven dance system is a scored search over a library.

The less obvious implication concerns AquaFlow's commercial position. The best open music models are often non-commercial, and no fountain show dataset exists for anyone to train on. A competitor's advantage is therefore unlikely to come from a better neural network. It will come from the scoring function, the site capability model, a simulator calibrated to real nozzles, and an A/B loop that turns every user's preferences into better weights. Those are assets AquaFlow can own outright, and the evaluation harness in Phase 0 is what makes them compound.
