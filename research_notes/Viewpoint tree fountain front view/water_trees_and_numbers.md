# Water trees / tree-shaped fountains and practical design numbers for a dense vertical-jet array (80–200 jets, 2–6.5 m, viewed from 14–20 m)

Research date: 2026-10-26. Note on method: about 19 search/fetch calls. Two OASE pages returned HTTP 429, so some vendor numbers come from search-result extractions of data sheets, not from a full read. Those numbers are flagged. All arithmetic marked "Inference" is mine, not sourced.

## 1. Real installations that form a tree with water: how they look and what equipment they use

### Takeaway
Documented commercial "water trees" are mostly **sculptural**: a stainless mast or branch frame with an effect nozzle on each branch tip (discs, sprays), or a single multi-orifice nozzle (palm, willow, pine) whose many small jets draw the outline. They read as a lit outline of separate effects, not a solid canopy. I found no well-documented example of a tree silhouette built from a large field of separate vertical jets of different heights in a pool, which is the approach being designed here.

### Cited Findings
- **Aquatic Show "Water Tree" (France):** a stainless-steel frame of slender tubular branches "arranged to evoke a fir tree": a central mast with progressively shorter branch tubes. Each tube's water hits a pellet (deflector) and makes a **water disc at the end of each branch**. Rental heights are 2.50 m and 3.50 m; units for sale come in custom shapes and sizes. Several can be grouped into a "forest". No flow or pump data is given. — [Aquatic Show – water tree](https://aquatic-show.com/en/our-services/water-tree)
- **Fountain People pine-tree effect:** a "pine-tree shaped water effect" in a "geyser-in-a-can" format that works regardless of water level. The housing is single-piece type 304 deep-drawn stainless steel, and the nozzles are brass, cast bronze or copper. — [LandscapeArchitect.com product listing](https://landscapearchitect.com/ladetails/landscape-product/fountains-equipment/fountain-people/geyser-in-a-cantrade)
- **Safe-Rain Palm Tree jet:** a set of **4 mm jets** angled so that front and back jets cross from any viewpoint. "On rising they look like the trunk of a palm tree and on falling like its leaves." Versions with 16, 20, 50 and 60 jets. Jet height 0.5–3.5 m, fall diameter 0.8–7.2 m, brass (chrome optional). No flow or pressure published on the page. — [Safe-Rain – Palm tree nozzle jet](https://saferain.com/en/fountain-equipment/fountain-nozzles/crystalline-fountain-jets/palm-tree-nozzle-jet/)
- **Safe-Rain Willow jet:** "molds water emulating the shape of the Willow tree". The water rises in a parabola to the outside and then falls. — [Safe-Rain – Willow tree jet](https://saferain.com/en/fountain-equipment/fountain-nozzles/crystalline-fountain-jets/willow-tree-jet/)
- **OASE Water Sphere (900/2500/3500):** stainless nozzle spheres where many fine jets meet into a "gentle sphere of water". They are designed to look like sculptures even when switched off. A possible "crown" or ball element. — [OASE water sphere 900](https://www.oase.com/en/fountain-technology/water-sphere-900-int)
- **Fountain built around a living tree (WaterShapes "Amazement in Motion"):** a 67 ft (about 20 m) water table with three concentric rings of jets around a planted tree, with groups of jets choreographed to vocals and instruments. The tree itself is real, not made of water. — [WaterShapes – Amazement in Motion](https://watershapes.com/amazement-in-motion/)
- **Chinese musical-fountain makers** (Himalaya, Huaxin, Huapu) list comet, flat fan, peacock, dandelion, mushroom, rotating and cascade nozzles, plus swing and variable-height jets. I found no specific "tree" effect described in text. — [Himalaya Music Fountain listing](https://www.aajjo.com/product/big-music-fountain-in-bursa-himalayamusicfountain); [Huaxin Fountain](https://www.infoconstruccion.es/empresas/huaxin-fountain)
- **Seasonal "Christmas tree" fountains** (e.g. Piatra Neamț, Romania) appear to be light structures on top of the fountain, not water shapes. — [Visit Neamț](https://www.visitneamt.com/?p=835)

### Inferences
- Commercial tree effects get their outline from **many small jets (4 mm) on one nozzle body** or **disc/spray effects on a branch frame**. A pool-scale tree from separate 12–25 mm vertical jets is closer to a musical-fountain "matrix" program (each jet height-controlled to draw a silhouette) than to any off-the-shelf water-tree product.
- Since commercial trees read as a lit outline of discrete effects, a jet-field tree will most likely look like "many jets forming a shape" up close and only merge at the canopy, where spray heads widen. See section 2.

### Gaps
- I found no reliable text or press source describing the jet layout of a Chinese "tree of life" or "pine tree" musical-fountain program, or of Dubai, Las Vegas or Korean tree effects. These exist mainly as videos without technical descriptions, and I could not review videos.
- No flow or pressure data was found for the Aquatic Show tree or the Safe-Rain palm and willow jets.

## 2. Visual merging at 14–20 m: how close jet heads must be to read as one mass

### Takeaway
Visual acuity is **not** the limiting factor. At 14–20 m, 1 arcminute is only about 4–6 mm, so any gap of tens of centimetres between jet heads is easily seen when there is contrast. Jets read as one mass only when their **spray envelopes physically overlap in the viewer's line of sight**. That happens through actual overlap (spacing ≤ head width) or through **staggered depth rows** that fill each other's gaps in projection, helped by mist and backlighting that lower gap contrast.

### Cited Findings
- The normal human minimum angle of resolution is about **1 arcminute** under ideal conditions. It comes from foveal cone spacing of about 0.5 arcmin (Nyquist limit). — [University of Sydney – Visual acuity theory](https://ophthalmology.sydney.edu.au/visualacuity_theory/index.html); [EyeWiki – Visual acuity testing](https://eyewiki.aao.org/Visual_Acuity_Testing_in_Adults)
- Separable gap s = d·tan(1′) ≈ d × 2.91×10⁻⁴ (standard geometry). — [Cyberphysics – receptor separation](https://cyberphysics.co.uk/topics/medical/Eye/receptorSeparation.html)
- Spray-type heads are made of light droplets that wind easily carries off. This affects how much a head spreads beyond its nominal width. — [Safe-Rain – spraying jet](https://saferain.com/en/fountain-equipment/fountain-nozzles/spray-feature/spraying-jet/)

### Inferences (my calculations)
- 1 arcmin at 14 m = **4.1 mm**; at 17 m = **4.9 mm**; at 20 m = **5.8 mm**. A 0.3 m gap at 17 m subtends about 1.0° (about 60 arcmin), which is 60× the acuity limit. Gaps are therefore visible unless contrast is low.
- Practical merge rule for one row seen face-on: **centre spacing ≤ head width** (0.2–1 m). For a "solid canopy" read, aim for about 0.7–1.0 × head width so envelopes overlap.
- With N staggered rows behind each other (offset by spacing/N), the projected spacing becomes spacing/N. For example, 3 rows at 0.9 m pitch give an apparent 0.3 m pitch, which merges with 0.3–0.5 m heads. This is the most water-efficient way to get a dense front look.
- Columns (the "trunk" region of plain vertical jets) are only 12–25 mm thick near the nozzle. At 17 m that is about 2.5–5 arcmin, so each one stays visible as a separate line. A trunk will look like a "bundle of lines" unless foamy or aerated jets are used.
- Contrast drivers: night uplighting from below lights the spray, and dark backgrounds make gaps between lit heads more visible. A mist or haze layer behind the jets lowers gap contrast and helps merging.

### Gaps
- I found no published fountain-industry "density" rule (jets per m or per m²) for continuous visual mass. This is usually handled by mock-up or simulation.

## 3. Hydraulics and power: flow and pressure per jet, totals, pumps, manifolds, VFDs

### Takeaway
A 12 mm clear or comet jet needs about **2.8 m³/h at 2 m, 4.1 m³/h at 4 m and about 5.5 m³/h at 6.5 m**, at 0.23–0.8 bar at the nozzle. An array of **80–200 such jets needs about 250–1,000 m³/h and roughly 15–50 kW of pump shaft power**. Foam (aerated) jets need roughly **3× more water** per jet at the same height.

### Cited Findings
- **OASE Comet 10-12 T (12 mm orifice):** about 31 L/min at 1 m / 0.11 bar; 46 L/min at 2 m / 0.23 bar; 68 L/min at 4 m / 0.48 bar; 98 L/min at 8 m / 1.00 bar. Wind-stable clear jet, water-level independent, ball joint up to 15° tilt. The Comet 10-14 T (14 mm) is about 143 L/min at 9 m. Values come from a search-tool reading of the OASE data sheet; the full PDF could not be fetched (HTTP 429). — [OASE Comet 10-12 T](https://www.oase.com/en/water-technology/comet-10-12-t-int); [OASE Comet data sheet PDF](https://www.oase-livingwater.com/fileadmin/dp/media/images-cb/DOK/PRD/DB/DOK_PRD_DB_50959-Comet3-5T_%23SEN_%23AQU_%23V1.pdf); [OASE Comet 10-14 T](https://www.oase.com/en-US/fountain-technology/comet-10-14-t-int)
- **Generic 1″ brass fountain nozzle (retail spec):** 60–180 kPa, 3.6–6.0 m³/h, height 2–7 m, spray diameter 0.3–0.8 m. Low-authority source, but it agrees with the OASE figures. — [ManoMano listing](https://www.manomano.fr/p/fountain-nozzle-internal-thread-connection-performance-is-stable-large-spray-volume-1-inch-garden-fountain-nozzle-210156287)
- **Safe-Rain spray jet G1″ with 10 mm outlet:** 1.0 / 1.5 / 1.85 / 2.0 m height at 11 / 16 / 19.5 / 21 L/min and 4.0 / 7.6 / 9.3 / 13.0 m water column. Spray heads need much more pressure per metre of height than clear jets. — [Safe-Rain spraying jet PDF](https://saferain.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/spray_fountain_nozzle_spraying-jet.pdf)
- **OASE Schaumsprudler 35-10 E foam jet (1″, stainless):** heights about 3.5–10 m. One column reads about 216 L/min (13 m³/h) at 3.5 m, rising to about 1,039 L/min at 10 m. The data-sheet extraction was ambiguous between water-type columns, so verify before use. — [OASE Schaumsprudler 35-10 E data sheet](https://www.teichfolie.de/media/products/Produktdatenblatt_Schaumsprudler%2035%20-%2010%20E%20.pdf)
- **Pipe practice:** economic velocity between pump outlet and nozzles is "generally accepted to be between 1 and 1.8 m/s". Suction pipe should be ≥1.5–2× the pump inlet diameter, with an eccentric reducer. For equal-height jets on a header, keep head loss per section low so every nozzle base sees similar pressure. For a graded profile, head loss per length can be matched to the slope of the height line. Avoid suction under-pressure to prevent cavitation. — [Safe-Rain – Tips of fountain design part 2](https://saferain.com/en/blog/tips-of-fountain-design-part-2/)
- **Pump power:** P = ρ·g·Q·H/η. Worked example: 75 m³/h × 30 m at 70% efficiency ≈ 8.75 kW. Power scales linearly with both flow and head. — [Grundfos ecademy – hydraulic fundamentals](https://www.grundfos.com/in/learn/ecademy/all-courses/the-extended-basic-hydraulic-principles-course-/an-introduction-to-basic-hydraulic-fundamentals); [calculate.co.nz pump power](https://www.calculate.co.nz/pump-power-calculator.php)
- Dayliff's catalogue gives height, flow and head tables per jet type and notes that display size depends on the flow and pressure pumps can supply. The text extraction was garbled. — [Dayliff fountain jets & nozzles PDF](https://dayliff.com/media/com_hikashop/upload/safe/fountain-jets-and-nozzles1.pdf)
- OASE notes that with geyser jets, "the pump selected… ultimately dictates the height of the water jet". — [Water Garden UK – Geyser jet](https://www.water-garden.co.uk/prod/geyser-t-fountain-nozzle)

### Inferences (my calculations from the figures above)
- **Per jet, 12 mm comet/clear:** 2 m ≈ 2.8 m³/h; 4 m ≈ 4.1 m³/h; 6.5 m ≈ 5.3–5.6 m³/h at about 0.8 bar (interpolated between 4 m and 8 m). For a tree profile (many low outer jets, few tall centre jets), a weighted mean of about **3.5–4 m³/h per jet** is reasonable.
- **Totals:** 80 jets ≈ 250–400 m³/h; 120 jets ≈ 420–500 m³/h; 200 jets ≈ 600–1,000 m³/h. If the canopy uses foam jets (about 13 m³/h each at 3.5 m), every 20 foam jets add about 260 m³/h.
- **Pump power:** total dynamic head of about 8–9 m at the nozzle plus 3–5 m losses ≈ 12–14 m. At η = 0.65: 400 m³/h × 13 m ≈ **22 kW**; 1,000 m³/h × 13 m ≈ **54 kW** shaft power. Low jets on separate pumps need less head (about 4–6 m), which saves 30–50%.
- **Pipe sizes at 1.5 m/s:** per-jet branch at 4 m³/h ≈ 31 mm ID (DN32); a 100 m³/h header ≈ DN150; a 400 m³/h main ≈ DN300.
- **VFD grouping:** group jets by height band / tree tier (e.g. trunk, lower canopy, mid, top crown), with one pump + VFD per tier. This matches the vendor advice to keep equal-height jets on a common low-loss header. Use a balancing valve (or orifice plate) on each jet branch to trim height within a tier, and per-jet solenoid or proportional valves only where individual animation is needed.

### Gaps
- No full OASE or Safe-Rain table for 16–25 mm clear jets was retrieved (the pages were rate-limited). Crystal Fountains and Longxin/Huaxin tables were not found.
- No vendor figure for typical system losses or for pump efficiency of fountain submersibles at these sizes.

## 4. Wind: limits, anemometer control, splash distance, basin margin

### Takeaway
Standard practice is an **anemometer with three programmable wind-speed stages** that lower height through the VFD, switch off the tallest jets, or shut down the pumps. Basin-margin rules of thumb range from **jet height ≤ distance to the nearest edge** (common) to **jet height ≤ ½ basin radius** (conservative). Still-air splash reaches about one jet height horizontally. Vendors publish no universal m/s thresholds; they are set on site.

### Cited Findings
- **OASE Wind-dependent fountain control 3 K:** anemometer-driven; depending on wind strength, "the fountain height can be controlled in three stages or switched off". Purpose: less spray onto passers-by and lower water loss. Controller IP54, anemometer IP33, 24 V DC. — [OASE wind-dependent fountain control 3 K](https://www.oase.com/en-US/fountain-technology/wind-dependent-fountain-control-3-k-int); [OASE anemometer K](https://www.oase.com/en/water-technology/anemometer-k-int)
- **Safe-Rain anemometer:** three programmable actions, each with a user-set wind speed, including modulating the frequency converter to limit splashing and switching pumps off. — [Safe-Rain anemometer](https://saferain.com/en/fountain-equipment/fountain-accessories/electrical/anemometer/)
- **Splash radius:** with no wind, horizontal splash reaches roughly **one jet height** from the jet. Outdoors, fit a wind gauge that shuts off the highest jets while the rest keep running. — [Safe-Rain – Tips of fountain design part 2](https://saferain.com/en/blog/tips-of-fountain-design-part-2/)
- **Basin rules:** "under normal wind conditions, the water spray height should not exceed the distance from the jet to the nearest edge of the pool". Delta also suggests a pool depth of at most 18 in. — [Delta Fountains](https://www.deltafountains.com/?p=772). Pool diameter about **2× spray height**. — [WaterShapes](https://watershapes.com/tag/raindrops). A more conservative design rule: limit jet height to **½ the pool radius**, i.e. diameter about 4× height. — [Student fluid-mechanics project (Scribd), low authority](https://www.scribd.com/document/819237298/CE223Project-Grp1C)
- Spray features with light droplets should stay low and avoid open, exposed sites. Larger orifices make larger droplets that drift less. — [Safe-Rain spraying jet](https://saferain.com/en/fountain-equipment/fountain-nozzles/spray-feature/spraying-jet/)
- Fit an automatic refill to replace evaporation, nozzle overspray and wind drift. — [Safe-Rain – Tips part 2](https://saferain.com/en/blog/tips-of-fountain-design-part-2/)
- The COMAP "Wind and Waterspray" problem frames the same logic: higher wind → lower flow and height, to keep spray inside the pool. — [COMAP – Wind and waterspray](https://www.comap.com/membership/member-resources/item/wind-and-waterspray)

### Inferences
- For a 6.5 m centre (crown) jet: at least **6.5 m** from that jet to the nearest edge (normal rule), or about 13 m (conservative). Outer 2 m jets need about 2 m. A tree layout with tall jets in the middle and low jets at the edges fits these rules well.
- Mist, fog and foam crown effects are the most wind-sensitive. They should be in the first anemometer stage, cut before the clear jets.
- Viewers at 14–20 m are outside the still-air splash zone even for 6.5 m jets, provided the basin edge holds the margin. Downwind drift is the main risk.

### Gaps
- **No sourced numeric wind thresholds (m/s)** for height reduction or shutdown were found. OASE and Safe-Rain leave them user-programmable, and their manuals were not retrieved. Do not invent defaults; set them on site or take them from vendor manuals.
- No sourced data on splash distance as a function of wind speed.

## 5. Maintenance and water treatment for many small nozzles

### Takeaway
Clogging is controlled mainly at the **pump suction** (large-area stainless suction strainers or grilles, mesh sleeves on submersibles), with the strainer opening **smaller than the smallest nozzle orifice**. Multi-orifice tree nozzles with 4 mm jets are the most clog-prone parts.

### Cited Findings
- Fountain suction strainers are baskets on pump suction lines that "prevent debris and particles from damaging pumps and clogging pipes & nozzles". Large-area stainless screens need less frequent cleaning. — [Fontana Fountains – suction strainers](https://fontanafountains.com/products/accessories-pool-fittings/suction-strainers/); [high-flow suction strainers](https://fontanafountains.com/products/accessories-pool-fittings/high-flow-suction-strainers/)
- Fine nylon mesh sleeves around pumps are used specifically to stop floating-fountain nozzles from clogging. — [Pond Experts – pump screen](https://www.pondexperts.ca/fish_food/Pump-and-Floating-Fountain-Screen-Protector-p443002143)
- The strainer should be slightly finer than the nozzle orifice. Finer mesh catches more but clogs faster and restricts flow. Persistent plugging usually points to an upstream cause. (From spray-equipment practice, transferable.) — [Sprayers101 – strainers](https://sprayers101.com/?p=7438)
- Suction areas should have a semi-rigid underwater grille, and pump suction submergence should follow the pump manual. — [Safe-Rain – Tips part 2](https://saferain.com/en/blog/tips-of-fountain-design-part-2/)

### Inferences
- With 12–25 mm jets, clogging is a lesser issue than with 4 mm multi-jet heads. Suction screens of a few mm, plus a side-stream sand or cartridge filter and sanitising (chlorine/UV) to control algae and scale, are typical pool or water-feature practice. Scale and biofilm on the orifice change the height and look of clear jets, so periodic descaling matters for a precise silhouette.
- Per-jet balancing valves drift as strainers load. Plan periodic re-trimming of the tree profile, or use pressure monitoring per tier.

### Gaps
- No fountain-specific filtration standard (turnover rate, micron rating) was retrieved from OASE, Crystal Fountains or Safe-Rain.
