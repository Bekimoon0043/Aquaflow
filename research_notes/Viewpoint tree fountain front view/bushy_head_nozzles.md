# Bushy / foamy / feathery jet-head nozzles and the physics of jet-top bloom

Scope: real nozzle types that can give a "fluffy lit head on a thin column" for a viewpoint-illusion tree fountain made of rows of vertical jets 2-6.5 m tall. The notes also cover the break-up and aeration physics that sets head size. Research date: 2026-10-26. About 17 tool calls were used, so the coverage is broad but not exhaustive. Several manufacturer sites (Fontana, OASE catalogue pageflip) returned 403/429 and could not be read.

## Q1. Which real nozzle types make a bushy head while the column below stays thin or clear, and what are their sizes, flows and pressures?

### Takeaway
No catalogue product is sold as "clear thin stem plus bushy head". Every aerated or foam family (foam jet / Schaumsprudler, ice tower, cascade, geyser, snowy jet) is white from the nozzle up. Their output is a thick white column that widens and "crowns" near the apex and falls back as a cone. Head diameters run about 0.2-0.3 m for 1" ice towers up to 1.5-2 m for 2-2.5" units. Flows are about 50-1000 L/min and pressures 0.1-2 bar for 1-10 m. Clear (laminar or crystal, comet) jets have the thin, clear stem but no natural bushy head. The closest buildable way to get "thin stem, fluffy top" is to rely on apex bloom of a turbulent jet (see Q3), or to mount a head nozzle (dandelion, crystal ball, sphere) on a thin riser pipe. The riser approach makes the "column" a solid pipe, not water.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- For the "thin column, fluffy head" look, the useful split is this. A foam jet or ice tower gives a bright, white, light-scattering head, but the column is white too, about 1-2.5x the nozzle body diameter and thickening upward. A clear or turbulent smooth-bore jet gives a thin, nearly invisible column, and its head comes only from apex bloom and break-up (Q3).
- Scattering from entrained bubbles makes aerated water very bright under uplight. The column is therefore likely to be as bright as the head unless the lighting is aimed or shuttered at the top. This is reasoning, not measured.
- A dandelion or crystal ball on a slim riser (DN25 at 0.6 m head, DN50-80 at 1.2-1.8 m head) is the only option where the head size is fixed by hardware rather than flow physics. But the "stem" is a pipe, and head height above the riser is only 0.6-2.3 m.
- Per jet for 2-6.5 m: a 1" ice tower uses about 130-200 L/min for a narrow (0.2-0.3 m) tower. A Schaumsprudler 55-15 E or 75-20 T uses about 250-900 L/min for 2-6.5 m (alignment caveat above). A row of 40+ jets therefore implies very large total pump flow (tens of m³/min) if foam jets are used.

### Gaps
- No measured apex head diameter vs height was found for any foam jet or ice tower. The "spray/coverage diameter" in Chinese listings is undefined.
- No data was found from WET Design, Hoffmann/Schleicher, Fountain People, Atlantic, Aquatique, Longxin, Huaxin, BEA/Beijing East Water or Wuhan Golden. The searches did not surface their datasheets, and OASE's catalogue page returned 429.
- I found no product sold as a "two-stage" or "nozzle-in-nozzle" jet with a clear core and a foamy top, and no "snowball", "rain tree", "mushroom/tulip on riser" or "jumping/leaping jet" datasheet with numbers in this pass.
- The OASE Schaumsprudler per-height alignment needs checking against the original PDF image.

## Q3. Physics: what controls jet break-up length and the bloom at the apex?

### Takeaway
Break-up regime depends on Weber number (We = ρU²d/σ), Reynolds number, density ratio and, crucially, nozzle-exit turbulence intensity Tu. Fountain jets of 2-6.5 m from 10-50 mm outlets have Re around 10⁵ and are in the turbulent regime. Turbulent jets break up at the surface over a length of order 10²·d. Laminar (straightened) jets stay intact far longer. At the apex of a vertical jet, gravity slows the flow to zero, so continuity forces the cross-section to grow, and air drag plus turbulence and surface break-up turn that region into a spray "crown". That is the physical origin of the bushy head.

### Cited Findings
- Trettel (2020) proposes a regime diagram built from about an order of magnitude more breakup-length data than earlier ones (22 pipe-jet studies). It renames the regimes: laminar Rayleigh, downstream transition (old "first wind-induced"), turbulent Rayleigh, turbulent surface breakup (old "second wind-induced") and atomization. Nozzle critical Reynolds number and turbulence intensity Tu₀ = (2k₀/3)^½/U₀ are added as variables. — [Trettel, Reevaluating the jet breakup regime diagram (engrXiv preprint)](https://engrxiv.org/preprint/download/740/version/1128/1657/1115)
- Laminar Rayleigh breakup length (Weber's theory): ⟨x_b⟩/d₀ = ln(d₀/2δ₀)·(We₀^½ + 3We₀/Re₀). Weber recommends ln(d₀/2δ₀) = 12, while Grant & Middleman (1966) found 13.4 fits better. For turbulent-Rayleigh jets the constant drops to about 3.3-4.8 (Phinney 1975; van de Sande & Smith 1976; Mansour & Chigier 1994). Exit turbulence therefore cuts the intact length by about 3x at the same We. — [Trettel](https://engrxiv.org/preprint/download/740/version/1128/1657/1115)
- Turbulent surface breakup regime (turbulent eddies perforating the free surface): ⟨x_b⟩/d₀ = 3.61·Tu₀^-0.275·We₀^0.334 (194 points, R² = 0.959, Kusui 1969 data with 5.4% ≤ Tu₀ ≤ 12.7%). Breakup onset is x_i/d₀ = 13.0·(Tu₀³We₀)^-0.915. The spray half-angle regression is tan(θ_i/2) = 5.32×10⁻⁵·We₀^0.605 (Faeth group data). The transition from turbulent Rayleigh to turbulent surface breakup is We₀,crit ≈ 8·Tu₀^-2. — [Trettel](https://engrxiv.org/preprint/download/740/version/1128/1657/1115)
- Transition criteria from Trettel: Re₀,crit about 2300 (start) and 4000 (fully turbulent) for pipe nozzles. For converging nozzles the critical value can be an order of magnitude or more higher. Jets that are laminar at the exit but go turbulent downstream ("bursting breakup") can break up more violently than fully turbulent ones. Eisenklam & Hooper and Hoyt & Taylor recommend tripping turbulence inside the nozzle to avoid this. — [Trettel](https://engrxiv.org/preprint/download/740/version/1128/1657/1115)
- Sallam, Dai & Faeth (AIAA-2002-1115) studied turbulent primary breakup of round and plane liquid jets in still air. — [Deep Blue record](https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/items/59aee679-16d7-4b67-be23-3d3d43ebef48/full)
- Sallam et al. (2006, crossflow) found turbulent-jet breakup is controlled by liquid turbulence above a "Faeth number" Fa = We_l·q^⅓ ≈ 17000, and by aerodynamics below it. — [Sallam et al. AIAA 2006-1517](https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/bitstream/2027.42/76872/1/AIAA-2006-1517-140.pdf); [arXiv review 2606.25125](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.25125)
- A study of breakup of a free-falling turbulent water jet in air exists (ASCE J. Hydraulic Eng.). Its content was not retrieved. — [ASCE](https://ascelibrary.com/doi/10.1061/%28ASCE%29HY.1943-7900.0001188)
- A fountain jet slows toward its top and its diameter grows with height. Air friction makes real fountains fall short of the Torricelli height. — [arXiv 0801.4160](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0801.4160)
- Safe-Rain: crystalline jets use flow straighteners to remove turbulence and air, while frothy jets deliberately generate turbulence or entrain air. — [Safe-Rain](https://saferain.com/en/fountain-equipment/fountain-nozzles/)

### Inferences
These are my own worked numbers, using standard formulas but not taken from a source.
- **Apex bloom from continuity.** For a vertical jet, U(z) = (U₀² − 2gz)^½. Constant flow gives d(z) = d₀·(1 − z/H)^-¼ with H = U₀²/2g. The jet is only 1.19x wider at 50% of its height, 1.41x at 75%, 1.78x at 90% and 3.2x at 99%. Nearly all the widening happens in the last few percent of the height. Water piles up there, and drag, turbulence and gravity spill it sideways into an umbrella or crown. This is why even a "plain" jet looks bushy at the top while the column stays thin.
- **Order of magnitude.** A 6.5 m jet needs U₀ ≈ 11.3 m/s ideal (more in practice). With d₀ = 20 mm water, We₀ = ρU²d/σ ≈ 1000·128·0.02/0.072 ≈ 3.5×10⁴ and Re₀ ≈ 2.3×10⁵. That is firmly turbulent. At Tu₀ = 5% the surface-breakup correlation gives x_b/d₀ ≈ 3.61·(0.05)^-0.275·(3.5×10⁴)^0.334 ≈ 3.61·2.28·33 ≈ 270, so x_b ≈ 5.4 m. Then:
  - A turbulent 20 mm jet will be shedding droplets from its surface over the upper part of a 6.5 m column, and fully broken near the top: a naturally bushy crown.
  - A straightened, laminar-exit jet of the same size keeps a clear column much higher.
  - Raising Tu₀ (rough or short nozzle, no straightener, aeration) shortens x_b only weakly (exponent -0.275). Raising We (higher speed, larger d) lengthens it.
- **Where to put the head.** For the "thin clear column + fluffy head" look, the physically consistent design is a smooth-bore or lightly turbulent (not laminar, not aerated) jet sized so the breakup length is about 0.6-0.8 of the jet height. Breakup onset then falls near the top third, which turbulent apex bloom amplifies. Very low Tu (crystal and laminar nozzles) suppresses the head. Aeration whitens the whole column.
- **Head diameter.** No validated relation for apex crown diameter vs height was found. As a first estimate, take the spray half-angle from the tan(θ/2) regression over the broken-up length near the apex, plus fall-back spread from drag. Chinese ice-tower listings (0.2-0.3 m for 1", 1-2 m for 2-2.5" at up to 10 m) bracket the realistic range. For jets 2-6.5 m tall a crown roughly 0.3-1.0 m across seems realistic for 20-50 mm turbulent/aerated jets. This is an inference to be verified by test.

### Gaps
- No sources were retrieved for McCarthy & Molloy (1974), Lin & Reitz (1998) or Grant & Middleman (1966) directly; their results are cited only through Trettel. No fountain-engineering book (for example a Crystal Fountains or OASE design guide) was retrieved.
- No published empirical correlation was found for the apex umbrella diameter of a vertical fountain jet vs height and outlet size.
- The correlations above come from horizontal or downward pipe jets. Their use for an upward, decelerating jet (U → 0 at the apex) is extrapolation.

## Q4. Which option gives the biggest, brightest head with the least visible column, and what head diameter is realistic for 2-6.5 m?

### Takeaway
No product combines "biggest bright head" with "least visible column". Aerated foam jets and ice towers are brightest but white all the way up. Clear or turbulent jets have an almost invisible stem but a smaller, sparser crown. For a viewpoint-illusion canopy the best compromise appears to be a plain turbulent (non-straightened, non-aerated) smooth-bore jet or a low-air foam jet, lit only near the top so the crown reads as the canopy. Head diameters of about 0.3-1.0 m per jet at 2-6.5 m are a realistic planning range, but this is unverified. The best-documented hardware options are OASE Schaumsprudler 55-15 E / 75-20 T (about 250-900 L/min per jet) and 1-2" ice towers (about 130-400 L/min, 0.2-1.6 m quoted spray diameters).

### Cited Findings
- Foam jets are aerated and white throughout. OASE quotes 125-1039 L/min and 0.11-1.95 bar across its larger models. — [OASE datasheet](https://www.teichfolie.de/media/products/Produktdatenblatt_Schaumsprudler%2035%20-%2010%20E%20.pdf)
- Ice tower DN25: 1-5 m height, 0.2-0.3 m diameter. Larger sizes reach 1.5 m diameter. A 1.5" listing claims a 1.6 m spread at up to 8 m. — [Aquaswan DN25](https://www.poolfountainaccessories.com/sale-28783239-dn25-dancing-fountain-nozzles-water-fountain-spray-heads-brass-ice-tower.html); [ManoMano 1.5"](https://www.manomano.fr/p/water-fountain-nozzle-head-spray-15-inch-internal-thread-brass-easy-to-install-ice-tower-nozzle-for-pond-and-fountains-210158727)
- Crystalline (laminar) jets are transparent and stable because straighteners remove turbulence and air. — [Safe-Rain](https://saferain.com/en/fountain-equipment/fountain-nozzles/)
- Dandelion heads give 0.6-1.8 m but are limited to 1-2.3 m heights above the nozzle. — [chinamusicfountain.com](https://chinamusicfountain.com/portfolio/dandelion-fountain-nozzle/)

### Inferences
- Ranking for "big bright head / least visible column" (reasoned, not measured):
  1. A turbulent clear jet with apex-only lighting: thinnest stem, crown about 0.3-0.8 m.
  2. A foam jet at modest air: brighter, larger crown, but the column is white.
  3. An ice tower: tapered white tower, the column is the effect.
  4. A dandelion on a riser: large defined head, but the stem is a pipe and the height is limited.
- Lighting matters as much as the nozzle. Aerated water scatters strongly, so a narrow-beam fixture aimed at the top 0.5-1 m of the jet will make the head read while the stem stays dark.
- Wind will widen and smear apex crowns. OASE calls foam jets "largely wind-stable" (a marketing claim), but no measured wind data was found.

### Gaps
- No measured luminance or brightness comparison between foam, ice-tower and clear-jet heads.
- No product-level data from WET, Hoffmann/Schleicher or the major Chinese musical-fountain makers was retrieved. A head-diameter vs height test is needed before committing to a design.
